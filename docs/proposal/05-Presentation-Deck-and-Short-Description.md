## B. Short description (for the meeting invitation or the email)

**One paragraph**

> Our website is where an exporter in Guangzhou, a customs officer in Baghdad, a buyer in Mumbai or a procurement team in Riyadh first decides whether to trust us. The current site was right for the company of 2024; we have grown past it. It hides half its pages, has no way to check a certificate, no page per office, and of nine accreditation logos only three can be checked in a public register, while our strongest verifiable asset, an active ANAB ISO/IEC 17020 accreditation covering five offices, is not shown at all. This 20-minute presentation sets out what the new website will say and do, three early design directions to react to, how it will be built on Wagtail (a free Python system) with planning budgets, a plan that goes live inside two months of approval, and the nine decisions only leadership can take. Nothing has been designed or coded; we agree the content first, then design, then build.

**Three sentences**

> The current ticqc.ae hides half its pages, loads slowly on phones and shows accreditation logos that cannot be checked in a register. The proposal rebuilds it around six promises: find any service in two clicks, prove every claim with a number and a link, speak the visitor's language when an office asks for it, a human on WhatsApp, a named owner per page, and fast on a cheap phone. I need nine decisions from leadership and your comments on the full documents within three weeks; the site can be live about six weeks after approval.

**One line (calendar subject)**

> TIC website rebuild: proposal round 1 and the decisions we need (20 min)
