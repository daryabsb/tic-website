# Service-line page benchmarks: what the best peers publish for each TIC service line, and what ticqc.ae has today

Research date: 2026-10-21. Researcher notes for the CONTENT side of the TIC Quality Control website proposal. Written for the directors and managers who own each service line. Every fact carries a source link; anything I concluded myself is under "Inferences" and labelled as such. Where a peer page could not be opened, I say so and name the substitute I used.

How to read these notes
- "Page anatomy" means the order of blocks on the page (headline, intro, service list, proof, downloads, form, FAQ, related links).
- "Proof points" means anything a visitor can check: an accreditation body named, a standard number, a count of offices, a sample report.
- "CTA" means call to action: the button or link that asks the visitor to do something ("Send us a request", "Book now").
- "Gated" means the visitor must give their email before a download opens.
- The TIC side of each comparison comes from the earlier audit note (ticqc-current-site-audit.md, section 2) plus four fresh fetches made today (About, Sustainability, the tiles product page, and the page sitemap).

Method and limits
- Pages were fetched live on 2026-10-21. sgs.com pages were read through the r.jina.ai proxy because sgs.com blocks plain fetchers; the proxy returned the page text but not always the heading levels, so where I say "H1 (inferred)" the heading level was not visible.
- The web-search budget for this session ran out after the first two rounds. All later pages were reached by direct URL. Guessed URLs that returned 404 are listed at the end so nobody repeats them.
- Camin Cargo (bot wall), AmSpec sub-pages, Applus+ service sheets, Halal Quality Control, ISWA, the Saudi Halal Center, TÜV Austria's halal page, Bureau Veritas UAE, DNV's assurance sub-pages and Bureau Veritas sustainability pages could not be opened. Substitutes are named per line.
- Word counts are estimates of the main body copy, excluding menus and footers.

## Service line 1: Marine cargo and vessel surveys (draft survey, ullage, bunker survey, sealing, on/off-hire, hold cleanliness, hose test)

### Takeaway
The majors do not give marine surveys a single long page; they give each survey type a short, plain page (250–450 words) with the exact survey name as the headline, one paragraph on why it matters, the standard it is done to, and a direct request link. SGS is the best reference because it names the standards (ISO 13739 for bunker quantity, ISO 8217 for fuel quality) and sells a proprietary tool; Control Union is the best reference for a short "how a draft survey is done" explanation. TIC's Marine page already uses the right vocabulary but has none of the depth, proof or conversion elements.

- SGS "Marine Vessel Inspections": H1 "Marine Vessel Inspections"; promise "Reduce risk when chartering vessels to move commodities"; sections: intro, "Our marine inspection solutions" (draft surveys, bunker surveys, on-hire/off-hire, cleanliness inspections, condition assessment), "Why choose SGS", six related-service cards (damage surveys, e-certificates, loading and discharge supervision, cleanliness, quality and quantity, draft surveys, pre-shipment, sampling), news, contact. About 350–400 words. No standards named on this page, no downloads, no FAQ; CTAs "Contact" (pre-filled as a quotation request) and "Send us a message"; utility links "Verify SGS Documents". ### Inferences (content checklist for TIC's marine pages)
- Keep a short Marine hub plus one page per survey type (Draft survey, Bunker survey, On/off-hire and condition survey, Hold and tank cleanliness, Sealing, Hose test, Ullage/outturn). Each sub-page: exact survey name as the headline; one paragraph "what it is and who orders it" (charterer, owner, trader, P&I club); "how we do it" in 3–5 steps; the standard or protocol followed (ISO 13739 for bunker quantity, ISO 8217 for fuel quality, the draft survey protocol); what the client receives (report, quantity agreement, photographs, seal numbers); ports and offices covered; "Request a survey" form that captures vessel name, port, ETA, cargo, survey type.
- Vocabulary to align with: "bunker quantity survey (BQS)", "on board quantity (OBQ/ROB)", "on-hire/off-hire survey", "cleanliness inspection", "loading and discharge supervision", "draft survey report", "quantity agreement". TIC's "Carrier Cleanliness Inspection" is not a term any peer uses; "hold cleanliness inspection" is.
- Proof points TIC can add without inventing anything: the ANAB ISO/IEC 17020 accreditation with scope wording, ports and hours of coverage, a sample survey report (redacted), the TIC Council compliance code (as Saybolt does).
- A document-verification link ("verify a TIC report by number") is a visible trust feature on SGS and Saybolt and absent on every mid-size peer except HQTS, Sunchine, QIMA and V-Trust (see regional note); it would set TIC apart in this tier.

### Gaps
- No peer page gives a draft-survey turnaround time or a price; that must come from TIC operations.
- AmSpec's marine services sub-page and Camin Cargo's site could not be opened (Camin shows a bot-verification wall); Camin's page anatomy is therefore not benchmarked.

## Service line 3: Agri-commodities and GAFTA/FOSFA superintendence (sampling, weight, quality, fumigation supervision, loading/discharge)

### Takeaway
GAFTA itself defines the service in plain words ("supervise the loading and discharge of traded agricultural commodities, including sampling, measurements, and quality and condition assessments") and tells traders to "look for the logo" and cross-check the online register. Cotecna's agriculture page is the best commercial template (services, products, labs, two ungated brochures, service-specific CTAs); Intertek's is the best for proof (lists GAFTA, FOSFA, USDA, FDA, AOCS, TQSA by name). TIC holds the GAFTA Approved Superintendent logo but its Commodities page is a duplicated carousel and its Agri page is a stats block with an unverified lab claim.

### Cited Findings
- GAFTA Approved Register of Superintendents: an applicant must be a Category C member, hold either a certificate of conformance to the "Gafta Standard for Supervision, Sampling and Check Weighing" or "ISO 17020 accreditation as a Type A Inspection Company with the appropriate scope", operate under Gafta 123 (Weighing) and 124 (Sampling) Rules and follow the register's Code of Practice; audits are on-site for first-time applicants and every five years, with annual audits generally remote; approved superintendents "may use Gafta Approved logos to promote services that match their membership and approved status"; downloads: Superintendent Code of Practice 2022, Gafta Standard for Supervision guide (2019), audit application form (2023), three sampling guides for Sampling Rules No. 124 (contracts from 1 July 2026); complaints go in writing to the Director General at ApprovedRegisters@gafta.com. — [GAFTA Superintendents register page](https://www.gafta.com/approved-registers/superintendents/?category=SUPR)
- GAFTA's flyer defines the Register of Superintendents as "for those businesses who supervise loading and discharge operations including sampling, measurements, and quality and condition assessments"; "It is a requirement of all standard Gafta contract forms that these services are performed by Gafta Approved members"; traders are told the Membership Directory "is updated in real time, please ensure you cross reference your chosen supplier to ensure their approval is up to date" and "Look for the Logo"; the flyer positions the Gafta Standard as "often a less complicated, cost effective alternative to ISO accreditation, especially for smaller businesses". — [GAFTA Approved Registers flyer (PDF)](https://www.gafta.com/media/psgdllwr/gafta_ar_2pp_a4_flyer_04022021.pdf)
- GAFTA Sampling Rules No. 124 (2025 edition) and contract forms (e.g., No. 18, effective 1 July 2026; No. 88 and 89, effective 1 October 2025) are published as PDFs on gafta.com; the search summary states that under the Sampling Rules superintendents must operate free from commercial, financial or other pressures and that if one party refuses to draw samples the other may call a superintendent from a different listed company (search snippet, not verified against the PDF text). — [GAFTA Sampling Rules 124 (PDF)](https://www.gafta.com/media/l0cejvxm/124_2025.pdf); [GAFTA Contract 18 (PDF)](https://www.gafta.com/media/dmyhdmsp/18_2026.pdf)
- Cotecna agriculture: H1 "Agricultural Inspection & Testing"; about 1,000 words; sections: intro, Services (Cargo Inspection, Draft Survey, Fumigation, Physical and Chemical Testing, Stockpile Monitoring), Agricultural Products (Cereals, Oils and Feed, Pulses, Rice, Cotton, Cocoa, Sugar, Wood Pellets), six named group labs with locations and external links, two ungated brochure PDFs (Agriculture 2026, 15.7 MB; Horticulture, 2.12 MB), "Why Choose Cotecna", news, eight digital-solution tiles; numbers "140+ offices and laboratories", "50+ countries", lab turnaround "8 to 24 hours, including weekends"; service-specific CTAs "Get Inspection Support", "Talk to Our Survey Experts", "Request Fumigation Support", "Get Testing Solutions", "Optimize Inventory Visibility", plus "SEND US A REQUEST"; no FAQ; no accreditation named ("internationally recognised accreditations and certifications"); the words superintendence, supervision of loading/discharge, GAFTA and FOSFA do not appear. — [Cotecna Agriculture](https://www.cotecna.com/en/services/agriculture)
- Intertek agriculture: H1 "Agriculture Solutions"; H2 "Global leadership in agricultural commodities assurance and certification"; about 300 words; sections: intro (ATIC: Assurance, Testing, Inspection, Certification), "Expert Product Managers at Your Service", "Global Respect and Accreditation" naming ISO, GOST, TQSA, USDA, FDA, FOSFA, GAFTA, IFT, AOCS, SAL and "accredited by the TIC Council" (sic), four ATIC solutions, twelve product lines, video, lab spotlight, contact block with 13 regional numbers including UAE, brochure "Agricultural Inspection Services" (PDF); CTAs "Send us a request", "Email Us" (web.agri@intertek.com), "Call a specialist", "Download the Brochure"; no FAQ. — [Intertek Agriculture Solutions](https://www.intertek.com/agriculture/)
- Control Union's draft survey and commodity sub-pages (see line 1) cover Sampling and sealing, Weight and quality determination, Pest control (fumigation), Stock monitoring, Temperature monitoring, Laboratory services; its testing page states that "most" of its labs are accredited by or approved under FOSFA, QS, FEDIOL, NOFOTA, GMP+, GAFTA, GlobalGAP, VERNOF and NEN. — [Control Union Draft survey](https://www.controlunion.com/service/inspection/commodity-inspections/draft-survey/); [Control Union Testing Services](https://www.controlunion.com/service/testing/)

- TIC today: the Commodities page (about 400 unique words) lists minerals, agricultural (wheat, rice, corn, oilseeds, sugar, cotton), metals, electrical, textiles, hard goods and toys, cosmetics, with scopes loading/offloading supervision, quality/quantity/weight control, sampling, packing & marking, visual inspection, damage surveys; its Metals, Textiles, Hard Goods and Agricultural sections repeat three times each (carousel leftover). ### Inferences (content checklist for TIC's agri-commodities page)
- Split agricultural commodities out of "Commodities & Consumer Products": it is a different buyer (grain traders, shippers, banks) and a different proof set (GAFTA register, FOSFA, APEDA).
- Page order: headline "Agricultural commodity inspection and superintendence"; promise in GAFTA's own words (supervision of loading and discharge, sampling, weighing, quality and condition); "Services" as separate blocks (Supervision of loading/discharge, Sampling and sealing, Check weighing / draft survey, Quality and condition assessment, Fumigation supervision, Stock monitoring / collateral, Laboratory analysis); "Commodities" grid (wheat, rice, corn, oilseeds, sugar, cotton, pulses, feed); "Approvals" block that states GAFTA Approved Superintendent with membership category and a link to the GAFTA directory entry so a trader can cross-check, APEDA registration with scope, ANAB ISO/IEC 17020 with scope; "Rules we work under" (GAFTA 123 Weighing, 124 Sampling, FOSFA rules if applicable); ports and offices covered (Gandhidham, Dubai, Lahore, Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur, São Paulo as applicable); brochure PDF; "Request superintendence" form (commodity, quantity, port, vessel, date, buyer/seller role).
- Logo use: GAFTA permits the Approved logo only for services matching the approved status; the website should show it next to the superintendence service, not as a generic homepage badge, and should link it to the register.
- Lab claims: say which laboratory, where, and who accredits it; "NABL" is an Indian accreditation body, so the statement needs the lab's name and certificate number (see line 5).

### Gaps
- Whether TIC Quality Control is currently listed in the GAFTA Membership Directory under UAE was not verified (the register listing itself is behind a search page that was not fetched).
- FOSFA's own guidance pages were not fetched; FOSFA membership status of TIC is unknown.
- Cotecna's and Intertek's agri pages do not publish fees or turnaround; no peer example exists for those.

## Service line 4: Consumer-product pre-shipment inspection (IPC, DPI, FRI, container loading, factory audit) and the eight product-specific pages

### Takeaway
The consumer-product inspection market has settled on one page anatomy: service acronym as the headline (PSI, IPC, DUPRO, CLC), a "what is it / when / what is checked" sequence, the sampling standard named (ISO 2859-1 / ANSI-ASQ Z1.4, AQL), a sample report download, a booking or quote form with product and country fields, a long FAQ, and a visible report-verification link. QIMA and Pro QC are the best references; HQTS gates its sample report behind an email. Product-specific pages add value only when they carry product-specific checkpoints, named test standards and defect lists (V-Trust's fabric page); TIC's new tiles page is already close to that bar but lacks a sample report, a form and acceptance criteria.

Mid-size peers earn trust on the About page with four things TIC's page lacks: a founding year and milestone timeline (HQTS: 1987 to 2024), named accreditations with body and number (Baltic Control: ANAB 9230, DANAK 5016, DANAK 7512), a clear ownership and group statement (Control Union "family company", Kiwa "part of SHV" with an impartiality sentence, Baltic Control "subsidiary of Apave Group"), and a downloadable corporate profile (Cotecna brochure and infographic). Leadership names are rare in this tier (Control Union names only its CEO; Cotecna, Kiwa, HQTS none), so TIC need not publish a full leadership page, but it must publish history, group and accreditations. TIC's About page has no founding year, no leadership, no group structure, a careers form, 14 addresses, and typos.
