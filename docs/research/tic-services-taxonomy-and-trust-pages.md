## Key question 1: How the TIC majors name and group their services

### Takeaway
The majors converge on a short, globally understood top layer: Testing, Inspection, Certification, plus Audit/Assurance, Training, Consulting/Advisory and (increasingly) Digital/Verification. Beneath that they organise either by industry (Bureau Veritas, Intertek) or by business line (SGS). "Assurance" is the umbrella word Intertek and SGS use for audit-and-certification style services; "Government & Trade" / "Government Services" is the standard label for PVoC/CoC programmes.

### Cited Findings
- Intertek's navigation lists core service categories "Assurance", "Testing", "Inspection", "Certification", with additional services "Auditing", "Consulting", "People Assurance", "Training", "Sourcing", "Business Assurance" and a separate "Total Sustainability Assurance" offering. — [Intertek Industries page](https://www.intertek.com/industries/)
- Intertek's government-programme business is branded "Government & Trade Services" and is organised by region/country (Africa, Americas, Asia, Middle East, Customs Union) and by solution family: "Government Trade Facilitation", "Tradeable and Customised Solutions" (Customised Inspection and Surveying, Factory Audits, Supplier Verification, Trade Finance Support, Warehouse and Stock Verification, Loading and Discharge Supervision, Supply Chain Risk Assessment, Container Inspection, Production Line Inspection, Waiver Certificate), "Smart Trade Solutions" (E-Customs, Digital Trade Facilitation, Customs Services) and "Verification and Advisory" (PPP, Global Market Access, Export Promotion Scheme). Intertek writes out "Certificate of Conformity" and "Verification of Conformity" rather than the abbreviations CoC/VoC on this hub page. — [Intertek Government & Trade](https://www.intertek.com/government/)
- Bureau Veritas group site has no top-level Testing/Inspection/Certification menu; "Services & Solutions" is organised by market: Agri-Food, Automotive & Transport, Buildings & Infrastructure, Consumer products & retail, Cybersecurity, Commodities, Cross-Market Services (e.g., "Certification & management services", "Industrial Product Certification"), Marine & Offshore, Oil & Gas, Power & Utilities. Footer links: Legal notice, Terms of use, Cookies policy, Personal data protection policy, Accessibility statement. — [group.bureauveritas.com](https://group.bureauveritas.com/)
- Bureau Veritas runs its government-programme business on a separate portal, Verigates, "a web platform dedicated to pre-shipment inspection programmes", with a flat country list, a Newsroom, online certificate requests and real-time file tracking. — [verigates.bureauveritas.com](https://verigates.bureauveritas.com/)
- SGS (site returns HTTP 403 to automated fetch; findings from search snippets and SGS's integrated report) reports its business as Connectivity & Products, Health & Nutrition, Industries & Environment and Natural Resources (testing and inspection) plus Business Assurance (previously "Knowledge") for certification. — [SGS 2023 Integrated Report](https://www.sgs.com/-/media/sgscorp/documents/corporate/reports-and-presentations/2020s/2023/sgs-2023-integrated-report-en.cdn.en.pdf)
- SGS service-page families found by search: "Certification and Audit" (pre-audit to certificate, supplier audits, sustainability assessments, consulting and training), "Digital Trust" (information security, cybersecurity, privacy, AI, cloud, advisory), "Verification & Assurance" (ISO 14064-1, 14067, 14068-1 assessments), "Import and Export Certification", "Product Inspection" (Initial Production Check, During Production Check, Final Random Inspection, Loading/Unloading Supervision), "Consulting". — [SGS Certification and Audit](https://www.sgs.com/en/our-services/certification-and-audit); [SGS Verification & Assurance](https://www.sgs.com/en-us/our-services/verification-and-assurance); [SGS Import and Export Certification](https://www.sgs.com/en-us/services/import-and-export-certification); [SGS Product Inspection](https://www.sgs.com/en-us/industry/consumer-products-and-retail/product-inspection)
- SGS names its country programmes "<Country> Product Conformity Assessment (PCA)" or "<Country> PVoC Program" (e.g., Qatar PCA, Mongolia PCA, Oman PCA). — [SGS Qatar PCA](https://www.sgs.com/en/services/qatar-product-conformity-assessment-pca); [SGS Mongolia PCA](https://www.sgs.com/en-mz/services/mongolia-product-conformity-assessment-pca); [SGS Oman PCA](https://www.sgs.com/en-bh/services/oman-product-conformity-assessment-pca-services)
- TÜV SÜD (site blocks fetch) describes itself as offering "testing, certification, auditing, inspection and advisory services" across eight industries. — [TÜV SÜD Our business](https://www.tuvsud.com/en/about-us/our-business)
- TÜV Rheinland labels the government business "Government Inspections and International Trade" and routes visitors by "Exporting to Africa / Asia / the Middle East" and "Choose your country of destination". — [TÜV Rheinland Iraq ICIGI page](https://www.tuv.com/landingpage/en/government-inspections-international-trade/navigation/pvoc/exporting-to-the-middle-east/iraq-icigi/)
- Cotecna files country programmes under "Verification of Conformity" with the authority name as the sub-page (e.g., /verification-of-conformity/egypt/goeic). — [Cotecna GOEIC page](https://cotecna.com/en/services/verification-of-conformity/egypt/goeic)
- GAFTA's own service vocabulary for commodities work is "supervision of loading and discharge, including sampling, measurements and quality and condition assessments" (Superintendents), "analysis" (Analysts) and "fumigation" (Fumigators). — [GAFTA Approved Registers](https://www.gafta.com/approved-registers/)

### Inferences
- A globally legible top layer for TIC would be: Inspection | Testing | Certification | Audit & Assurance | Government & Trade Services (PVoC/CoC) | Training | Advisory | Digital. This mirrors Intertek's labels, which are the most explicit of the majors.
- "Government Services Programs (GSP)" on ticqc.ae is close to industry vocabulary; "Government & Trade Services" or "Verification of Conformity (PVoC/CoC)" would be recognised instantly by exporters searching for Intertek/BV/Cotecna/TÜV equivalents.
- Marine survey work (draft, ullage, bunker, sealing, hose test) sits under "Inspection" (cargo & marine surveys) at the majors, not under an industry; GAFTA supervision belongs with agri-commodity inspection.
- TIC's "Business Assurance" label matches SGS/Intertek usage, but at those firms it means management-system certification and audits; using it for halal + CSUAS only will read as narrow.

### Gaps
- sgs.com and tuvsud.com return HTTP 403 to automated fetching, so their live top-level menu labels could not be captured verbatim; the SGS labels above come from individual service pages and the integrated report. A manual browser check is recommended before copying labels.
- Applus+ and DNV navigation was not captured.

GAFTA commodities); Oil, Gas & Petrochemicals; Chemicals; Consumer Products & Retail; Automotive & Vehicles; Marine & Shipping; Construction & Building Materials; Industrial & Manufacturing; Metals & Minerals; Government & Public Sector. ## Key question 3: Catalogue of government conformity programmes in TIC's regions, what exporters look for, 2026 status and approved bodies

### Takeaway
There are roughly 25–35 active PVoC/CoC-type programmes across the Middle East, Africa and Asia; the majors each list 20–35 of them on a dedicated government-services hub. For TIC's footprint the key active ones are Iraq ICIGI (COSQC), Egypt GOEIC, Saudi SALEEM/SABER, Kuwait KUCAS, Qatar, Oman, UAE MoIAT, Libya CoI, Botswana SIIR, Morocco VoC, Algeria, Nigeria SONCAP, Kenya PVoC (new 2026–2029 cycle), Tanzania/Zanzibar, Uganda, Ethiopia ECAE, Mongolia. Exporters look for the same nine things on every programme page, and the Bureau Veritas datasheet template is the cleanest model of that structure.

### Cited Findings — what exporters look for (page anatomy at the majors)
- Bureau Veritas datasheets follow a fixed nine-section template: 1. Regulation (authority, notification date, start date, appointed companies, scope, assessment basis); 2. Products subject (groups, exempted, prohibited, minimum value); 3. Technical requirement (standards, national deviations, labelling, other specific requirements); 4. Application (applicant, application name "Request for certificate (RFC)", required documents, application offices); 5. Control testing requirements; 6. Inspection requirements; 7. Facilitation procedures (routes); 8. Conclusion of the assessment (CoC, release note, discrepancy note); 9. Fees. — [BV Iraq VOC datasheet Rev 6.7 May 2024](https://verigates.bureauveritas.com/sites/verigates/files/2019-02/Iraq%20-%20Datasheet_0.pdf)
- Cotecna's GOEIC page sections: programme title and intro; regulatory update notice; "Egypt Conformity Assessment Process" with a process diagram; "Egypt GOEIC Product List" card; "Contact Us" (free quote); "Related Documents" with four PDFs ("Verification of Conformity (Infosheet)", "Request for Certification Form", "VOC Contact List", "Complaints & Appeals Guidelines"); "Why Choose Cotecna"; "YOUR BENEFITS"; "Latest News"; newsletter sign-up. No fee schedule or FAQ on-page (FAQ is linked externally). — [Cotecna GOEIC](https://cotecna.com/en/services/verification-of-conformity/egypt/goeic)
- Intertek's Iraq page sections: programme name; "The requirements for Iraq are:"; "To apply for a Certificate of Conformity, you will need to supply Intertek with:" (RfC form, proforma invoice, product data sheet, production info such as lot/expiry, evidence of compliance such as test reports/halal/health/phytosanitary/free-sale certificates, transport document, L/C, certificate of origin); "FAQs about the Iraq CAP Programme"; "Resource Center: Iraq" (RfC PDF form, "Advice on Trading with Iraq" flyer, Exporter and Importer Guidelines, Regulated Product List April 2025, Intertek offices contact details March 2025); "Need help or have a question?" with phone numbers per region, email and a contact form; links to Inview remote inspection and FastTek expedited certification. — [Intertek Iraq CoC](https://www.intertek.com/government/product-conformity/exports-to-iraq/)

### Cited Findings — programme-by-programme (status as found in October 2026)
Iraq (ICIGI / "VOC Iraq", COSQC)

Egypt (GOEIC CoI/CoC, ACID)
- Cotecna "GOEIC-accredited since 2012"; programme began with leather and textiles, expanded in 2016; photovoltaic solar systems added from 17 August 2026; Route A for infrequent shipments, Route B for regular shipments; physical inspection, sampling, ISO 17025 testing and factory audits. — [Cotecna GOEIC](https://cotecna.com/en/services/verification-of-conformity/egypt/goeic)

## Key question 4: Mandatory or expected public web content for accredited bodies (required by standard vs common practice)

### Takeaway
The standards require surprisingly little to be published proactively: only ISO/IEC 17021-1 requires public, unrequested information (audit and decision processes, schemes, use of marks, complaints/appeals handling, impartiality policy). ISO/IEC 17065, 17020 and 17025 require information to be "available on request", with 17065 leaving the certified-product directory to the scheme. Everything else on a trust page (code of ethics, anti-bribery, privacy, terms, H&S, quality policy, certificate-verification tool) is accreditation-body rule, TIC Council commitment, law, or common practice, and should be labelled as such.

### Cited Findings — ISO/IEC 17021-1:2015 (management-system certification)
- 8.1.1 (required, proactive): the CB shall "maintain (through publications, electronic media or other means), and make public, without request, in all the geographical areas in which it operates, information about a) audit processes; b) processes for granting, refusing, maintaining, renewing, suspending, restoring or withdrawing certification or expanding or reducing the scope of certification; c) types of management systems and certification schemes in which it operates; d) the use of the certification body's name and certification mark or logo; e) processes for handling requests for information, complaints and appeals; f) policy on impartiality." — [IAS slides reproducing 17021-1 section 8.1](https://cdn-v2.iasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/17021-1-2015-Section-8-1.pdf)
- 8.1.2 (required, on request): "a) geographical areas in which it operates; b) the status of a given certification; c) the name, related normative document, scope and geographical location (city and country) for a specific certified client." Note 2: "The certification body can also make the information in 8.1.2 public by any means it chooses without request, e.g. on its internet website." 8.1.3: information including advertising shall be accurate and not misleading. — [IAS slides](https://cdn-v2.iasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/17021-1-2015-Section-8-1.pdf)
- DAkkS reads 4.6/8.4.3 strictly: publishing certificate data without a request procedure is possible only in non-security-critical areas and with the client's prior express contractual agreement. — [DAkkS on CertSearch](https://www.dakks.de/en/news/no-obligation-to-use-the-iaf-certsearch-database.html)
- IAF MD 28 (mandatory via IAF MLA accreditation bodies; transition completed 26 October 2024): CBs must upload or confirm certificate data to IAF CertSearch at least monthly; UKAS warned continued accreditation is in jeopardy for non-compliance; RvA rejected making participation a condition of accreditation. — [SCC bulletin on IAF MD 28](https://scc-ccn.ca/accreditation/bulletins/iaf-md-28-upload-and-maintenance-data-iaf-database-certsearch); [IAF CertSearch data management](https://support.iafcertsearch.org/certification-bodies/data-management); [UKAS technical bulletin Oct 2024](https://ukas.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/2024.10.07-Technical-Bulletin-Activation-of-CertSearch-account.pdf); [RvA position](https://www.rva.nl/en/nieuws/rva-supports-voluntary-use-of-certsearch-database/)
- 5.2.5: the CB, any part of the same legal entity and any entity under its organisational control shall not offer or provide management-system consultancy. — [Continuum GRC summary of 17021](https://continuumgrc.com/what-is-iso-17021-and-certification-of-management-systems/)

### Cited Findings — ISO/IEC 17065:2012 (product certification, incl. halal/CoC schemes)
- 4.6 Publicly available information (required, on request): "The certification body shall maintain (through publications, electronic media or other means), and make available upon request, the following: a) information about (or reference to) the certification scheme(s), including evaluation procedures, rules and procedures for granting, for maintaining, for extending or reducing the scope of, for suspending, for withdrawing or for refusing certification; b) a description of the means by which the certification body obtains financial support and general information on the fees charged to applicants and to clients; c) a description of the rights and duties of applicants and clients, including requirements, restrictions or limitations on the use of the certification body's name and certification mark and on the ways of referring to the certification granted; d) information about procedures for handling complaints and appeals." — [ISO/IEC 17065:2012 (law.resource.org copy, text extracted locally)](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17065.2012.pdf)
- 7.8 Directory of certified products (required): the CB "shall maintain information on certified products which contains at least a) identification of the product; b) the standard(s) and other normative document(s) to which conformity has been certified; c) identification of the client. The parts of this information that need to be published or made available upon request in a directory ... are stipulated by the relevant scheme(s). As a minimum, the certification body shall provide information, upon request, about the validity of a given certification." Note: a scheme directory satisfies the requirement. — [ISO/IEC 17065:2012](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17065.2012.pdf)
- 4.1.3 Use of licence, certificates and marks: the CB shall control ownership, use and display of certificates and marks as the scheme specifies (Guide 23, ISO/IEC 17030 referenced); 4.1.3.2 misleading references found in publicity "shall be dealt with by suitable action" (Guide 27: corrective action, withdrawal, publication of the transgression, legal action). — [ISO/IEC 17065:2012](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17065.2012.pdf)
- 7.13 Complaints and appeals: documented process, acknowledgement, decision by persons not involved; 7.13.6 persons who provided consultancy to a client shall not be used in the complaint/appeal decision. No clause requires publishing the procedure on the web; 4.6 d) makes it available on request. — [ISO/IEC 17065:2012](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17065.2012.pdf)

### Cited Findings — ISO/IEC 17020:2012 (inspection bodies; TIC's ANAB accreditation)
- 4.1.1–4.1.5: inspection activities shall be undertaken impartially; the body shall not allow commercial, financial or other pressures to compromise impartiality; shall identify risks to impartiality on an ongoing basis (note: risks can arise from ownership, governance, management, personnel, shared resources, finances, contracts, marketing including branding, and sales commission); shall demonstrate how it eliminates or minimises identified risks; "shall have top management commitment to impartiality". The text does not require a public impartiality statement. — [ISO/IEC 17020:2012 (law.resource.org copy, text extracted locally)](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17020.2012.pdf)
- 4.1.6 and Annex A.1 (Type A, third-party): the body "shall be independent of the parties involved", shall not be engaged in design, manufacture, supply, installation, purchase, ownership, use or maintenance of the items inspected, and shall not be part of a legal entity so engaged; Note 1 allows exchanging technical information with the client "e.g. explanation of findings, or clarifying requirements or training". — [ISO/IEC 17020:2012](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17020.2012.pdf)
- 4.2.1 confidentiality: the body "shall inform the client, in advance, of the information it intends to place in the public domain". — [ISO/IEC 17020:2012](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17020.2012.pdf)
- 7.5.2 (required, on request): "A description of the handling process for complaints and appeals shall be available to any interested party upon request." 7.6.1–7.6.5 set the process elements (receive, validate, investigate, decide; track; acknowledge and report progress; decision by persons not involved; formal close-out). — [ISO/IEC 17020:2012](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17020.2012.pdf)
- GAFTA accepts ISO 17020 Type A accreditation as the route to the Superintendents register; ILAC P15:05/2020 is the mandatory application document for 17020 under the ILAC MRA. — [GAFTA registers flyer](https://www.gafta.com/media/psgdllwr/gafta_ar_2pp_a4_flyer_04022021.pdf); [ILAC P15 revision notice](https://ilac.org/?p=123133); [NATA 17020 application document](https://nata.com.au/files/2021/05/ISO-IEC-17020-Inspection-Standard-Application-Document.pdf)

### Cited Findings — ISO/IEC 17025:2017 (laboratories)

No proactive public-information clause equivalent to 17021-1 8.1 was found. — [PJLA complaints webinar slides](https://www.pjlabs.com/downloads/webinar_slides/7.25.2022_Complaints.pdf)

### Cited Findings — accreditation-body and ILAC/IAF rules on symbols and website claims
- ILAC P8:11/2023 §9.1: accredited CABs may claim accreditation on "website(s); publicity and advertising material; brochures and organisation publications; technical literature; business reports; quotations or proposals", but the AB's rules "shall require the use of the accreditation symbols and claims of accreditation status in a way that is not misleading as to which conformity assessment activities are actually covered by the CAB's scope of accreditation". §9.3: the claim must relate only to activities within scope ("In proposals or quotations, it is necessary to distinguish conformity assessment activities that are covered by the scope of accreditation from those which are not"); the symbol must not be affixed to products or imply the AB approves a product or accepts responsibility for results; symbol on letterhead must not be used for quotes/reports where none of the work is in scope. §7: endorsed reports containing out-of-scope results need a disclaimer such as "The conformity assessment activities marked * are not covered by the scope of accreditation." §11: misuse can lead to corrective action, suspension, withdrawal, publication of the transgression, legal action. — [ILAC P8:11/2023 (PDF, text extracted locally)](https://acreditare.md/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/ILAC_P8_11_2023.pdf)
- ANAB PR 1018 (Policy on Use of ANAB Accreditation Symbols and Claims of Accreditation Status): symbols may be used only after accreditation is granted; the ANAB logo itself may not be used by any other entity; legacy ANSI-ASQ symbols had to transition by 1 January 2025; section 5.1 limits the symbol to reports containing accredited information listed on the scope; a combined ANAB/ILAC mark is available on request; the policy is listed on ANAB's ISO/IEC 17020 document page. — [ANAB 17020 documents](https://anab.ansi.org/resource/iso-iec-17020-inspection-bodies-documents-resources/); [ANAB blog on symbols](https://blog.ansi.org/anab/use-of-accreditation-symbols-iatf-16949/); [ANAB combined mark request](https://anab.ansi.org/combined-mark-request); [PR 1018 excerpt (aic-builds mirror)](https://aic-builds.org/?p=3246)
- UKAS: the symbol may appear on websites only where "the extent and limitations of the accreditation held is clearly described", e.g., a disclaimer beside it, "Our accreditation is limited to those activities described on our UKAS schedule of accreditation", with a link to the published schedule; unaccredited results in reports must be clearly disclaimed; not for vehicles, buildings, flags or goods. — [UKAS brand website FAQs](https://www.ukas.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/UKAS-brand-website-FAQs.pdf); [UKAS symbol rules](https://ukas.com/customer-area/rules-for-the-use-of-the-ukas-logo-and-symbol/); [UK government conditions for use](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1022081/guidance-ukas-accreditation-logo-and-symbols-v2.pdf)
- EIAC (Dubai): the governing document is EIAC-RQ-GEN-002 "The Conditions for the Use of EIAC Accreditation Symbol and ILAC MRA/IAF MLA Marks" (copies hosted by BSI); Law No. (9) of 2024 defines the EIAC symbol. — [EIAC-RQ-GEN-002 rev 7](https://www.bsigroup.com/siteassets/pdf/ar-ae/about-us/eiac-rq-gen-002-rev-7-eiac-accreditation-symbol-ilac-mra-iaf-mla.pdf); [Dubai Law No. 9 of 2024](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/%5CLegislation%20Reference%5C2024/Law%20No.%20(9)%20of%202024%20Emirates%20International%20Accreditation%20Centre.pdf)
- MoIAT/ENAS: "EP-02 Conditions for the use of ENAS accredited symbol" rev 3 exists in the MoIAT open-data library. — [ENAS EP-02](https://moiat.gov.ae/-/media/site/moiat/open-data-document-library/2021/assessors-kit/ep-02-conditions-for-the-use-of-enas-accredited-symbol_rev-3.ashx)

### Inferences
- Required-by-standard web content for TIC, by scope: (17020 ANAB) nothing must be on the web, but the complaints/appeals description and the impartiality commitment must be available on request and the client must be told what will be made public; (17065 if TIC certifies products/halal) scheme rules, fee basis, rights and duties incl. mark use, complaints/appeals procedure available on request, plus a directory/validity confirmation as the scheme stipulates; (17021-1 if TIC ever certifies management systems) the six 8.1.1 items must be public and unrequested, and certificate data must flow to IAF CertSearch monthly.

## Key question 5: Halal certification body web expectations (UAE MoIAT/ESMA, GAC, JAKIM) and certificate verification norms

### Takeaway
In the UAE, a halal certification body must be registered with MoIAT (Cabinet Resolution 10/2014; UAE.S/GSO 2055-2) on the basis of accreditation from an approved body such as GAC, and MoIAT publishes a public directory of registered bodies with accreditation body, dates and status. Recognised bodies therefore need a web page that states registration/accreditation (with certificate links), scope, the standard applied, and a certificate look-up or verification channel; SGS Gulf's halal site uses a "Track Your Halal Certificate" number search.

### Cited Findings
- MoIAT registration of halal certification bodies: "official recognition ... of acceptance of halal certificates issued by registered halal certification bodies" under Cabinet Resolution No. (10) of 2014; requirements are a valid trade licence and a valid accreditation certificate with scope from an accreditation body approved by the Ministry; fees paid online; page last updated June 2021. Renewal is a separate service. — [MoIAT registration service](https://qc.moiat.gov.ae/en/services/registration-of-halal-certification-bodies); [MoIAT renewal service](https://moiat.gov.ae/services/renewal-of-registration-of-halal-certification-bodies)
- MoIAT's National Accreditation Department registers HCBs after verifying compliance with approved halal standards (UAE.S 2055-2). — [MoIAT open data halal](https://opendata.moiat.gov.ae/en/halal)
- MoIAT Halal Service Directory lists approved accreditation bodies for halal, registered halal certification bodies and approved slaughterhouses; each entry shows accreditation body, issue and expiry dates, status, with links to registration and accreditation certificates; cached copies show statuses changing between snapshots (e.g., an entry "Active" in one copy and "Expired" in another). — [MoIAT Halal Service Directory](https://www.moiat.gov.ae/en/open-data/halal) (returned 404 on direct fetch during this research; content from search index)
- GAC accredits halal CBs against GSO 2055-2:2021 (e.g., SGS Gulf Limited's GAC certificate). — [SGS GAC accreditation certificate](https://www.sgs.com/-/media/sgscorp/documents/corporate/brochures/sgs-hn-gac-accreditation-certificate-en.cdn.en-JO.pdf)
- Under the Emirates Conformity Assessment Scheme (Module H) for halal products, the certificate is valid 3 years from issuance and the Halal National Mark is the approved UAE mark. — [SGS MoIAT halal datasheet](https://sgs.com/-/media/sgscorp/documents/corporate/brochures/sgs-afl-halal-certification-uae-datasheet-a4-en-hr.cdn.en-BH.pdf)
- SGS Gulf's halal certification directory site has a "Track Your Halal Certificate" search by certificate number and an "All Certificate" listing link, and states MoIAT approval in the UAE and SASO/SFDA recognition in Saudi Arabia. — [SGS halal certification directory](https://halalcertificationdirectory.sgs.com/)
- JAKIM recognised the UAE's halal products control system, national certificates and national halal mark managed by ESMA; JAKIM's recognised foreign body list stood at 88 bodies in 49 countries after an October 2024 amendment; a secondary source cites a list effective 30 January 2026 (unverified against halal.gov.my). — [Salaam Gateway on JAKIM/UAE](https://salaamgateway.com/story/malaysias-jakim-recognises-uae-halal-products-control-systems); [RTM on JAKIM additions](https://berita.rtm.gov.my/highlights/senarai-berita-highlights/senarai-artikel/jakim-recognises-five-additional-foreign-halal-certification-bodies/); [Halal Foundation](https://halalfoundation.org/?p=66053)
- ESMA/MoIAT recognition MoUs with foreign accreditation bodies exist (e.g., ESMA recognising KAN-accredited bodies' halal certificates). — [Salaam Gateway on KAN](https://salaamgateway.com/story/uaes-recognition-of-indonesias-national-accreditation-committee-halal-certificates-to-harmonise-stan)
- No search result links "TIC Quality Control" to MoIAT halal registration; the directory itself could not be opened to check. — [search on TIC halal registration](https://halalfoundation.org/halal-certification-uae/)

### Inferences
- A credible halal page needs: the registration/accreditation body named with certificate number and link to the MoIAT directory entry; the standard (UAE.S/GSO 2055-2, GSO 2055-1 for products); scope (slaughter, food processing, cosmetics etc.); the process and timeline; mark-use rules; a certificate verification route (number search or email); and the list of markets where the certificate is recognised (UAE, GCC via GAC, Malaysia via JAKIM if applicable).
- ISO/IEC 17065 4.6 and 7.8 apply to halal product certification, so scheme rules, fee basis, rights/duties and a validity-confirmation route must be available on request; a public directory is the common practice (SGS, MoIAT).

### Gaps
- TIC's actual halal registration/accreditation status (MoIAT, GAC, EIAC) must be confirmed internally; not visible from public search.

## Key question 6: How majors present Training and Consulting without undermining impartiality

### Takeaway
The majors keep training and consultancy visible but fenced: a consultancy ban for management-system certification (17021-1 5.2.5), training limited to generic content, a two-year cooling-off between consultancy/internal audits and certification, separate business units or brands, impartiality committees, and explicit disclaimers on training pages.

### Cited Findings
- ISO/IEC 17021-1 5.2.5: the CB and any entity under its organisational control shall not offer or provide management-system consultancy; a two-year wait applies before certifying a system on which the CB provided internal audits or consultancy. — [Continuum GRC summary](https://continuumgrc.com/what-is-iso-17021-and-certification-of-management-systems/); [EA FAQ on consultancy](https://european-accreditation.org/sp_accordion_faqs/question-38-18-consultancy-on-one-ms-and-certification-on-another-one/)
- ACCREDIA guidance: training is not consulting but is listed among threats to impartiality (5.2.3 note); where a course relates to management systems or auditing it is confined to generic information, and "the trainer should not provide client-specific solutions"; explaining certification criteria, identifying improvement opportunities and explaining methodologies is acceptable. — [ACCREDIA CIG recommendations](https://www.accredia.it/app/uploads/2016/10/6704_CIG_Recommendations__ISO_IEC_17021.pdf)
- Intertek (Hong Kong training page): "Intertek does not provide consulting services for management systems certification. Any consulting activities provided by Intertek are separate and independent of certification activities." — [Intertek HK training](https://w3inte.intertek.com.hk/training)
- Bureau Veritas Certification: impartiality policy monitored by internal and external impartiality committees; auditors sign no-conflict contracts and the Code of Ethics; Impartiality Risk Analysis covers relationships across BV business units. — [BV Certification impartiality](https://certification.bureauveritas.com/impartiality)
- USB Certification (non-major example): an auditor who has delivered internal training for an organisation cannot audit it for two years; consultancy for certification is excluded. — [USB Certification impartiality policy](https://fr.usbcertification.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/21.-Impartiality-Policy.pdf)
- ISO/IEC 17020 Annex A.1 Note 1: Type A independence "does not preclude exchanging technical information between the client and the inspection body (e.g. explanation of findings, or clarifying requirements or training)". — [ISO/IEC 17020:2012](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17020.2012.pdf)
- ISO/IEC 17065 7.13.6: personnel who provided consultancy to a client shall not be used to decide that client's complaints/appeals. — [ISO/IEC 17065:2012](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17065.2012.pdf)
- Intertek is also accredited (Intertek Italia) to certify learning-service providers under ISO 29993/29994 and approved by IACET as a continuing-education provider, i.e., training is positioned as a credentialed service line. — [Intertek Italia ISO 29993](https://www.intertek.com/news/2022/03-28-intertek-italia-first-accredited-for-certification-of-learning-services-according-to-iso-29991-iso-29993-and-iso-29994/); [Intertek IACET](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230424005174/en/Intertek-Approved-as-an-Accredited-Provider-by-the-International-Accreditors-for-Continuing-Education-and-Training)

### Inferences
- TIC's Training page should carry a standing impartiality note (generic training only; no client-specific solutions; training does not influence inspection or certification outcomes) and Consulting/advisory should be presented as separate from any certification and inspection scope, ideally under a distinct name.
- Because TIC is a Type A inspection body, any "advisory" must stay clear of design, supply or maintenance of inspected items (Annex A.1 b).

### Gaps
- Verbatim text of 17021-1 5.2.3 Note 1 and 5.2.5 from the standard itself was not retrieved (only secondary reproductions).

## Key question 7: Good examples of exporter-facing "how it works" pages and downloadable document checklists

### Takeaway
The best patterns are Cotecna's process diagram plus four-document download block, Intertek's requirements/document checklist plus resource centre and FAQ, TÜV Rheinland's download bundle (forms, guide, brochure in English and Arabic, fee structure, exporter undertaking) plus webinars and gazette alerts, and Bureau Veritas's structured datasheet and Verigates portal with online requests and file tracking.

### Cited Findings
- Cotecna GOEIC: process diagram under "Egypt Conformity Assessment Process"; product-list card; "Related Documents" (Infosheet, Request for Certification Form, VOC Contact List, Complaints & Appeals Guidelines); regulatory-update notice at the top; news and newsletter. — [Cotecna GOEIC](https://cotecna.com/en/services/verification-of-conformity/egypt/goeic)
- Intertek Iraq: explicit "you will need to supply Intertek with" checklist (RfC form, proforma invoice, product data sheet, production info, evidence of compliance incl. halal/health/phytosanitary/free-sale certificates, transport document, L/C, certificate of origin); FAQ link; Resource Center with RfC form, flyer, exporter/importer guidelines, regulated product list (April 2025) and office contacts (March 2025); regional phone numbers and contact form; cross-sell of remote inspection (Inview) and expedited certification (FastTek). — [Intertek Iraq CoC](https://www.intertek.com/government/product-conformity/exports-to-iraq/)
- Intertek maintains a "Regulatory News & Updates" feed per country (e.g., Egypt ACID notices). — [Intertek Egypt regulatory news](https://www.intertek.com/government/export-import/regulatory-news-updates/news-egypt/egypt-rpl-acid-number/)
- TÜV Rheinland ICIGI: downloads (Application for certification, Application for registration, Government Inspections and International Trade Guide, ICIGI brochure EN/AR, ICIGI Fee Structure, Undertaking by exporter), recorded webinars, "Regulatory updates" gazette alerts, office finder, and a "Choose your country of destination" selector by continent. — [TÜV Rheinland ICIGI](https://www.tuv.com/landingpage/en/government-inspections-international-trade/navigation/pvoc/exporting-to-the-middle-east/iraq-icigi/); [TÜV Rheinland Kenya PVoC](https://www.tuv.com/landingpage/en/government-inspections-international-trade/navigation/pvoc/exporting-to-africa/kenya-pvoc-program/)

### Inferences
- A TIC programme-page template that matches or exceeds the majors: (1) regulatory summary with authority, legal basis and TIC's appointment status; (2) who must comply and regulated product list (downloadable, dated); (3) routes (A/B/C or registration/licence) with a decision helper; (4) step-by-step process diagram with typical timeline; (5) document checklist with RfC form download; (6) testing and inspection requirements incl. labelling/national deviations; (7) fees or "fee structure PDF"; (8) FAQ; (9) regulatory updates feed; (10) contacts per office and a request-a-certificate form; (11) complaints & appeals link; (12) revision date and disclaimer.
