## 1. Top-level menu, mega-menu structure, Services vs Industries split, breadcrumbs, site search

### Takeaway
All six sites use four to six top-level items, and every one of them separates "what we do" (Services) from "who we do it for" (Sectors/Industries/Segments); the cleanest models for a 13-office company are Kiwa (one "Solutions" mega-menu with five columns: popular solutions, areas of expertise, industries, services, standards) and Element (one "Industries & Services" mega-menu with two columns). Breadcrumbs are universal; site search is always present but usually hidden behind an icon.

### Cited Findings
- DNV header: "Sectors", "Services", "Insights", "About Us" plus "Sign in to Veracity"; Sectors = Maritime, Energy, Healthcare, Medical devices, Food and beverage, Aquaculture and ocean health, Automotive and aerospace, Rail, Other industries; Services = Advisory, Certification, Classification, Cybersecurity, Data and analytics, Inspection, Software, Testing, Training, Verification and assurance, each linking to /services/ with a type filter (e.g. "Certification" = /services/?types=2644) — [DNV homepage](https://www.dnv.com/) and [DNV services](https://www.dnv.com/services/)
- DNV /services/ is a searchable catalogue with a "Search" box, "Relevance" sort, pagination to page 72, and cards showing banner image + sector label + "Service" type label + title + truncated description; no visible filter panel on the page itself — [DNV services](https://www.dnv.com/services/)

### Inferences
- Four patterns exist for the Services/Industries split: (a) two separate top-level items (DNV, LR, UL); (b) one combined mega-menu with two columns (Element); (c) one "Solutions" umbrella containing services, industries and standards (Kiwa); (d) services grouped under sectors inside one Services menu (Applus+). For TIC's current structure (Industries + Services already split) the Element or Kiwa combined mega-menu would cut one click and let a visitor arrive by either route.
- Kiwa's "Standards" column (ISO 9001, 22000, 45001 etc.) is a direct match for how TIC customers search for Halal, ISO or CoC schemes; it is a cheap addition to a mega-menu.
- Applus+ shows what happens without a single global information architecture: the UAE site menu differs from the global one, so a visitor who switches country loses orientation.

### Gaps
- Mega-menu visual layout (columns, imagery) could only be inferred from link order; screenshots were not available.
- The number of DNV services was not stated (approximately 72 pages of results).

## 2. Country/region/language handling and local office pages

### Takeaway
Three models appear: (1) separate country domains or sub-sites with their own content (DNV: 33 local sites; Applus+: /{country}/{lang}/ paths with 36 hreflang tags; UL: 27 regional sites; Kiwa: "Kiwa Global (English)" plus country sites with a country-suggestion cookie), (2) a single global site with an office/location finder (Element: ~159 locations with region tabs, map and list; LR: office & port finder), and (3) a mixed model (LR: main site plus /tr/ and lr.org.cn). For 13 offices, the Element/LR single-site-plus-finder model is far cheaper to maintain than per-country sites.

### Cited Findings
- DNV: a "Global" control opens "Local websites (regions and countries)" with 33 entries, most on separate domains (e.g. dnv.de, dnv.co.uk, dnv.no, dnv.cn, "Africa - French" and "Africa - English" as separate entries); the office locator page offers a "country or location" field and five region tabs (World, Europe, America, Africa & Middle East, Asia, Australia & New Zealand) but states no office count — [DNV office locator](https://www.dnv.com/contact/find-our-offices/)
- DNV Maritime states "~180" maritime offices and "65+" countries — [DNV Maritime](https://www.dnv.com/maritime/)
- DNV GL consolidated more than five CMSs onto SDL/Tridion in 2017 to support "more than 36 local websites in 18 languages" (older platform; the current site is Optimizely, see section 10) — [RWS case study](https://rws.com/content-management/tridion/resources/dnv-gl-case-study); [SDL press release 2017](https://prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/sdl-powers-dnv-gls-digital-online-transformation-646372163.html)
- LR: language selector offers Turkish (/tr/) and Chinese (external lr.org.cn); "Office & Port finder" at /en/about-us/office-finder/; footer links "LR China website" and "LR Türkiye website" — [LR homepage](https://www.lr.org/en/)
- An agency case study for LR's earlier platform cites "78 markets all requiring localised, specialist content", built-in translation/localisation and geo-targeting — [Dept case study (2020)](https://figarodigital.co.uk/?p=12404)
- Applus+: URL pattern /global/en/ and /ae/en/; the UAE site shows "UAE" beside the logo and has its own "UAE Offices Map", "Offices in UAE", "Head Offices" (/ae/en/contact-us/offices) and "Locations Directory" (/ae/en/contact-us/offices-country); the global homepage states "+58 countries" under "International presence"; the homepage HTML carries 36 hreflang alternates — [Applus+ UAE NDT](https://www.applus.com/ae/en/what-we-do/services/ndt); [Applus+ global](https://www.applus.com/global/en/); raw HTML scan of https://www.applus.com/global/en/ (this research)
- Applus+ "Vehicle Inspection" links out to 12 national sites (e.g. applusiteuve.com for Spain) rather than hosting them — [Applus+ global](https://www.applus.com/global/en/)
- UL: locations page is a static hierarchy of 6 regions > 37 countries/territories > ~180 entries; each entry shows city + type "(Laboratory)" or "(Office)", street address, "Tel:" / "Customer Service Tel:", a "Contact:" line, a "Map" link to Google Maps and sometimes a "Local Site" link (e.g. canada.ul.com); UAE entries: Abu Dhabi laboratory in "Mussafah, ICAD" and a Dubai office at "Dubai Science Park"; KSA: Riyadh office; footer "Choose a region" lists 27 regional sites with languages (e.g. Canada English and Français Canadien, Vietnam English and Tiếng Việt) — [UL locations](https://www.ul.com/about/locations)
- UL contact form asks "Where do you live?" (country) and "What is your preferred language?" (11 languages) — [UL contact](https://www.ul.com/contact-us)
- Kiwa: hero states "Kiwa is represented in over 35 countries" with a "Find your location" CTA; header label "Kiwa Global (English)"; contact page says "offices in over 35 countries across Europe, Asia, the Americas, and Oceania" and links "Head office locations" (/en/about-kiwa/head-office-locations/); the site sets a "CountrySiteSuggestion" cookie — [Kiwa homepage](https://www.kiwa.com/en/); [Kiwa contact](https://www.kiwa.com/en/contact/); raw header scan of https://www.kiwa.com/en/ (this research)
- Element: language selector lists English, Deutsch, Español (elementmaterialsmexico.com), Français, Italiano, Korean (elementkorea.kr), Svenska, Vlaams; locations page has "List View" / "Map" toggle, region tabs (All, The Americas, Europe, Africa, Asia, Middle-East), ~159 entries at /locations/{region}/{slug}, each with name, country and short description including accreditation (e.g. Aberdeen "a UKAS and ISO/IEC 17025 accredited laboratory"); Middle East entries include Abu Dhabi - Umm al Naar, Al Futtaim Element Dubai - Dubai Investments Park 1 and 2, Dubai - Barsha Heights, Dammam, Jeddah, NEOM, two Doha entries; "Saudi Arabia" is misspelled "Saudia Arabia" — [Element homepage](https://www.element.com/); [Element locations](https://www.element.com/locations)
- Element shows regional toll-free numbers including "Middle East: +971 800 353 6368 (Toll free from UAE)" on the homepage and contact page — [Element contact](https://www.element.com/contact-us)

### Inferences
- Per-country sites (DNV, Applus+, UL) require a dedicated editorial team per market; the Applus+ UAE site has a different menu to the global one, which shows the drift risk. TIC with 13 offices should prefer one global site with a location finder that filters by region/country and shows address, phone, email, services offered and accreditations per office (the Element entry model plus the UL address/phone/map detail).
- Arabic (and possibly Chinese, Portuguese, Thai) language versions are a translation decision, not a separate-site decision; LR shows you can run two or three languages off one main site.

### Gaps
- Kiwa's actual country list and local domains could not be read (head-office page URL guessed wrong; the correct page was found but not fetched within the call budget).
- DNV's office-locator entry format (address/phone/map) was not visible in the text extraction.

## 4. Certificate / mark verification tools

### Takeaway
Five of the six run a public, no-login verification tool: DNV Certificate Checker (search by company or certificate number, QR on every certificate), LR E-Certificate Verification (Unique Tracking Number) and LR Ships in Class (IMO number/ship name/flag), UL Product iQ (free account; file number/product/category/model) plus UL Verify (marketing-claims database), Kiwa Certificate Finder (company name or certificate number, minimum three characters) with a "Certificate Verification and Fraud Reporting" page. Element has no global checker, only a Singapore certificate checker. For TIC (CoC certificates, Halal certificates, inspection reports) a checker is the single most credibility-building tool to add.

### Cited Findings
- DNV: the public tool is certificatechecker.dnv.com; it lists valid management system and product certificates issued globally and locally, returns only the first 15 entries so users are told to refine by company name and country/city, also accepts certificate number, explains that a company may be missing while initial certification or recertification is "awaiting technical verification", and states that every certificate carries an embedded QR code that opens a lookup in DNV's secure certificate storage — [search summary of DNV sources](https://certificatechecker.dnv.com/); [DNV traceable trusted certificates](https://www.dnv.us/assurance/traceable-trusted-certificates/); [DNV QR code guide PDF](https://africa.dnv.com/siteassets/images/pdf-documents/qr-code-guide.pdf). Direct fetch of certificatechecker.dnv.com returned only the title "CertChecker" (JavaScript app), so field labels were not read.
- DNV Maritime links a public "Vessel register" at https://vesselregister.dnv.com/vesselregister and a "Customer tools" page (/maritime/customer-tools/) — [DNV Maritime](https://www.dnv.com/maritime/)
- LR E-Certs: heading "Verify documents using their unique tracking number"; one field "Unique Tracking Number (UTN)" and a "Verify document" button; guidance PDFs for "UNKNOWN" signature status, a user guide for IMO-compliant electronic certificates, and "eCerts FAQs – Flag States and Clients" (dated 2026-02-04) — [LR e-certs](https://www.lr.org/en/client-support/ecerts/)
- LR Ships in Class: an online register of ships and assets classed by LR, searchable by IMO number, ship name or flag, with a PDF of "All LR class suspensions, withdrawals, transfers and changes in the last year" and a link to the LR Foundation heritage centre for pre-1998 ships; no login stated — [LR Ships in Class](https://www.lr.org/en/about-us/who-we-are/lr-ships-in-class/)
- "Class Direct" does not appear on the LR homepage or classification page; the current names are "Client Portal" (portal.lr.org) and "Digital Workbench" — [LR homepage](https://www.lr.org/en/); [LR classification](https://www.lr.org/en/services/classification-certification/)
- UL Product iQ: free account required on first use; search by UL file number, product type, category code or model number; replaced the Online Certifications Directory; upgraded accounts add saved searches, tagging, notifications and UL confirmation letters; authorities having jurisdiction get the upgrade free — [UL Product iQ](https://productiq.ulprospector.com); [UL Product iQ Q&A](https://latam.ul.com/en/news/introducing-ul-product-iq-database-qa); [IAEI column](https://iaeimagazine.org/columns/ul-question-corner/ul-question-corner-introducing-the-ul-product-iq-database/). Direct fetch of productiq.ulprospector.com/en returned HTTP 403.
- UL Verify: "a public database of verified marketing claims", "View all claims" at https://verify.ul.com/search; UL Verification is "an objective, science-based assessment that confirms the accuracy of marketing claims" — [UL Verify](https://verify.ul.com/)
- UL also links a "Marks and Label Hub" (markshub.ul.com), the "Sustainable Products of Trust (SPOT) Database" (spot.ul.com) and a fraud-report route "Make a report" to market-surveillance.ul.com — [UL homepage](https://www.ul.com/); [UL certification](https://www.ul.com/services/certification); [UL contact](https://www.ul.com/contact-us)
- Kiwa Certificate finder: one search bar for "a company name or certificate number", "Enter at least 3 characters", no country/business-unit filters; footer link "Certificate Verification and Fraud Reporting" (/en/certificate-verification-and-fraud-reporting/); a "Certified companies" callout appears on the Certification overview page and a "Certificate finder ... Check it now" block sits on the homepage — [Kiwa certificate finder](https://www.kiwa.com/en/services/certification/certificate-finder/); [Kiwa homepage](https://www.kiwa.com/en/)
- Applus+: the global homepage highlights a "certified products directory (ISO 17065, Spain only)" — [Applus+ global](https://www.applus.com/global/en/)
- Element: no global checker; the portals page lists "Singapore Test Services Certificate Checker" (certificationsingapore.element.com) and "Metech Instrument Organizer (MIO)" for calibration certificates — [Element portals](https://www.element.com/elementdirect)

### Inferences
- The minimum viable TIC checker is the Kiwa model: one input (certificate number or company), public, with a fraud-reporting page beside it. The DNV QR-on-every-certificate pattern turns every printed CoC/Halal certificate into a link back to the site.
- For government CoC programmes (Iraq, Egypt), a verification page is also what customs officers and importers will look for; LR's "Flag States" FAQ shows that the audience for verification is regulators as much as clients.

### Gaps
- Field-level details of DNV Certificate Checker and UL Product iQ could not be read directly (JS shell / 403); descriptions rely on DNV's own guidance pages and UL's own announcements.
- Result-page layout of the Kiwa finder was not observed (no sample search performed).

### Inferences
- Borrow list for TIC: Kiwa's Solutions mega-menu with a Standards column; Element's location finder entry model with UL's address/phone/map detail; Kiwa/DNV certificate checker with QR codes on certificates; Kiwa's service page template (quote + contact carrying service ID, process steps, FAQ, downloads); Element's named regional experts; LR's "urgent survey request" route for Marine; UL's "How can we help you?" routing and consent handling; Applus+ "Get a quote" with service category parameter; a trust-numbers row and a grouped accreditations page that exceeds all six.
- Avoid list: per-country sites, hero carousels, PDF-only policies, hand-maintained lists, multiple portals, nine-item top navs, pages over ~300 KB of HTML, and missing accessibility statements.

### Gaps
- Brand colours/typography for five sites, Veracity and Product iQ internals, Applus+/Kiwa quote-form fields, Kiwa country list, LR office & port finder and DNV office entry formats remain unverified (see per-section Gaps).
