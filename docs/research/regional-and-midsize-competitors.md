Research date: 2026-10-21. Method: live fetch of each site (homepage plus the Iraq/Egypt programme page where one exists), web searches for the competitor set, and official regulator lists where findable. Sites that refused the fetch are flagged; for those I relied on search snippets only. Everything here is as observed on the date above and may change.

Sites fetched successfully: ticqc.ae, cotecna.com, controlunion.com, qima.com, hqts.com, balticcontrol.com, sunchineinspection.com, dashinspectorate.com, v-trust.com, proqc.com, geochemglobal.com, applus.com/ae, intertek.com (Iraq page only, as reference).
Sites that blocked or failed: geochem.ae (ECONNREFUSED), atcinspection.com and atcglobal.ae (DNS not found), nmi.ae (DNS not found), eil.ae (ECONNREFUSED), tcis.ae (ECONNREFUSED), gulftic.com (returned only a page title, no body: probably JavaScript-rendered), ae.tuvaustria.com (HTTP 500 on both pages tried).

## Key question 1: Which 8 to 12 companies are TIC's real website competitors, and why?

### Takeaway
TIC's real website competitors are the other COSQC/GOEIC/SASO-appointed mid-tier bodies that sell the same government programmes to the same exporters: Cotecna, HQTS, Baltic Control, Sunchine, Applus+ (UAE), TÜV Austria (UAE) and Gulftic for programmes; Control Union, Geo-Chem/Cotecna CPS, QIMA, Pro QC and V-Trust for commodity and consumer-product inspection. Several of the names in the brief (ATC, NMI, EIL, SIMPEX, Alpha Technical Services, Al Rukun) either have no reachable website or could not be identified at all.

### Inferences
- Recommended competitor set (12), in two tiers:
  - Direct programme competitors (same COSQC/GOEIC/SASO customers): Cotecna, HQTS, Baltic Control, Sunchine, Applus+ UAE, TÜV Austria UAE, Gulftic, DASH Inspectorate.
  - Service-line competitors (commodities, GAFTA, consumer-products PSI): Control Union, Geo-Chem/Cotecna CPS, QIMA, Pro QC and V-Trust (the latter two mainly for the China/Asia sourcing clients TIC serves from its China, India and Pakistan offices).
- The majors (BV, Intertek, SGS, TÜV Rheinland, Applus+) set the content standard for the Iraq page; the mid-tier set the realistic design and feature bar.
- A recent appointment surge (Baltic Control Aug 2024, Sunchine Sept 2024, TÜV Rheinland Mar 2025) means the Iraq CoC market is more crowded online than TIC's page implies; TIC's "appointed by COSQC" claim is no longer a differentiator by itself.

"Alpha Technical Services" matched only a US railcar firm; the only UAE "ATS" is "ATS Inspection and Certification Services LLC SPC" in Abu Dhabi (directory listing only: [Yellow Pages UAE](https://www.yellowpages-uae.com/ats-inspection-and-certification-services-llc-spc-201093)).
- ATC Global / ATC Inspection: neither atcinspection.com nor atcglobal.ae resolved, and no search result named them; I could not confirm the company or its domain.
- NMI and Emirates Industrial Laboratory: nmi.ae did not resolve and eil.ae refused the connection; not assessed. Vinçotte/Kiwa not assessed (it is a European major, not a regional CoC competitor).
- LinkedIn pages were not fetched (typically blocked for unauthenticated fetches).

## Key question 2: For each competitor, menu structure, programme presentation, verification, forms, portals, languages, chat, offices, accreditations, design quality and technology

### Takeaway
Every mid-tier competitor except TIC, Geo-Chem and DASH has at least one of: a client portal, a certificate/report verification tool, or a multi-language switcher; the better ones have all three. Almost all mid-tier sites are WordPress (HQTS, Control Union, Sunchine, Pro QC, TIC itself), so WordPress is not what makes TIC's site feel dated; the missing features and the template are.

## Key question 3: Which mid-size competitor has the best website and why? Which has the worst?

### Takeaway
Cotecna has the best mid-size site for TIC's purposes (government programmes, portal, certificate authentication, three languages, per-programme forms and guides); QIMA has the best user experience and technology but does not sell government programmes. DASH Inspectorate (Dubai) is the worst, and Geo-Chem is the most dated. TIC's current site sits in the lower-middle: strong programme FAQ content, but no portal, verification, languages or forms.

### Cited Findings
- Cotecna: Customer Portal, Certificate Authentication, EN/ES/FR, VoC programme pages each with a "Request for Certification Form (PDF)", "VOC Contact List (April 2026)", "Complaints & Appeals Guidelines", product-list page and an exporter guide — [Cotecna Egypt GOEIC](https://cotecna.com/en/services/verification-of-conformity/egypt/goeic); [Cotecna](https://www.cotecna.com/).

- Baltic Control: typed contact form, portal, programme cards, but typos and a footer email mismatch (text baltic@ vs mailto cert@) — [Baltic Control](https://www.balticcontrol.com/).
- DASH: typos, "#" links, counters at 0, forex trading on an inspection site, no accreditation bodies named — [DASH](https://dashinspectorate.com/).

### Inferences
Ranking of competitor websites (mid-size and regional set; 1 = best), judged on relevance to TIC's buyers (exporters, importers, commodity traders), feature completeness, content depth on programmes, languages, and polish:
1. Cotecna (cotecna.com): the model to copy for programme pages, downloads, portal and verification; weaknesses are no Iraq page and no chat.
2. QIMA (qima.com): the model for UX, onboarding ("Find your service"), portal/apps and languages; irrelevant for government programmes.
3. HQTS (hqts.com): the closest like-for-like (WordPress/Elementor, Dubai office, Iraq CoC page, 10 languages, certificate lookup); let down by sloppiness.
4. Control Union (controlunion.com): clean, credible, strong portal story and country sites; no programmes or verification.
5. Baltic Control (balticcontrol.com): best programme information architecture after Cotecna, portal, typed enquiry form; dated CMS and copy errors.

## Key question 4: Which site features are common across competitors and therefore expected by customers?

## Key question 5: How do competitors handle Iraq CoC (COSQC / ICIGI) and what do exporters expect to find?

### Takeaway
The majors' Iraq pages (Intertek, Applus+) and the better mid-tier ones (Baltic Control, HQTS) all give the same skeleton: who appointed them, product categories, a document checklist, a 3 to 4 step process from RFC to border verification, downloads (RFC, product list, contact list, guidelines), and a request button; TIC's page already has the richest FAQ (23 questions) and both registration routes, but is weak on download format, timelines, border-verification explanation, naming consistency and an online form.

### Cited Findings
- Programme background exporters look for: Intertek "Since 1 January 2020, exporters and importers trading with Iraq must comply with the Pre-Inspection, Testing and Issuing of Certificates of Conformity Programme"; noncompliance risks "severe delays in goods clearance, penalties, or even shipments being returned" — [Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/government/product-conformity/exports-to-iraq/). HQTS: "adopted in late 2019 and took effect in January 2020"; COSQC requires a CoC for "over 1,000 products across twelve categories" — [HQTS](https://www.hqts.com/services/iraq-certificate-of-conformity/). The original ICIGI scheme started 1 May 2011 under COSQC Law No. 54 of 1979 — [Iraq Business News](https://www.iraq-businessnews.com/2011/07/07/pre-importation-inspection-program-for-goods-to-iraq/).
- Kurdistan has a separate regulator (KSQCA) and Intertek keeps a separate "Kurdistan Region of Iraq CoC" page — [Bureau Veritas](https://verigates-ppd.bureauveritas.com/node/105); [Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/government/product-conformity/exports-to-iraq/).
- Regulated lists change often: Bureau Veritas says lists are "revised and updated very frequently, at least twice per month" — [Bureau Veritas](https://verigates-ppd.bureauveritas.com/node/105); TÜV Rheinland announced an updated product-standards list effective 17 June 2025 — [TÜV Rheinland](https://www.tuv.com/press/en/press-releases/key-regulatory-update-under-iraq-icigi.html).
- Process presentation: Applus+ 4 steps (RFC with invoices, packing lists, HS codes, test reports "ISO 17025 reports where applicable"; conformity assessment; inspection at origin; verification at Iraqi border ports with a "Release Document") — [Applus+](https://www.applus.com/ae/en/what-we-do/service-sheet/certificate-of-conformity---iraq). Intertek 4 steps incl. destination inspection by "an Iraq-based Intertek authorised office" and random surveillance, plus registration for regular exporters — [Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/government/product-conformity/exports-to-iraq/). Baltic Control 3 steps ending in a "secured Certificate of Conformity (COC)" issued after receipt of final invoice and transport document, plus destination visual check — [Baltic Control](https://balticcontrol.com/inspection/governments-organizations/iraq). Sunchine: CoC plus a "Release Note" at Iraqi entry points — [Sunchine](https://www.sunchineinspection.com/iraq-coc/).
- Product categories listed in text: Intertek (food, toys, electrical/electronic, vehicle spare parts and tyres, chemicals, cosmetics and perfumery, tobacco, construction materials, kitchenware, stationery, baby care, textiles, lubricants, "not exhaustive") — [Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/government/product-conformity/exports-to-iraq/); Baltic Control (toys, electrical, food, cosmetics and cleaning agents, household hardware, chemicals, textiles and footwear, fuel-burning appliances, construction materials) — [Baltic Control](https://balticcontrol.com/inspection/governments-organizations/iraq); TIC (construction, food, toys, chemicals, electrical, tires, cosmetics, household appliances, "Others") — [TIC](https://ticqc.ae/iraq-coc/).
- Downloads: Intertek 5 items (flyer, exporter/importer guidelines, product list Apr 2025, office contacts Mar 2025, RfC) — [Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/government/product-conformity/exports-to-iraq/); HQTS authorization letter PDF and contact list — [HQTS](https://www.hqts.com/services/iraq-certificate-of-conformity/); Baltic Control flyer PDF — [Baltic Control](https://balticcontrol.com/inspection/governments-organizations/iraq); Applus+ PDF service sheet and process graphic — [Applus+](https://www.applus.com/ae/en/what-we-do/service-sheet/certificate-of-conformity---iraq); TIC Client Declaration (.docx), Request for CoC (.docx), company profile (PDF), approval letter (.jpg) — [TIC](https://ticqc.ae/iraq-coc/).
- TIC's page covers both routes (unregistered goods: RFC, review/testing, PSI, certificate, border inspection, NCR path; registered goods: Request for Registration, Statement of Registration, SoR monitoring, CoC) and 23 FAQs including the difference between "Iraq CoC Program" and "COSQC ICIGI CoC Programme" — [TIC](https://ticqc.ae/iraq-coc/).
- TIC page weaknesses observed: fees absent, timelines "vary", ICIGI never expanded, three names used for one programme ("Iraq VoC Program", "Iraq CoC", "COSQC ICIGI CoC Programme"), no Iraq-office phone or email, no online submission — [TIC](https://ticqc.ae/iraq-coc/).

### Inferences
- What an exporter expects on an Iraq CoC page, synthesised from the five pages: (1) a one-paragraph statement of the legal basis and the risk of shipping without a CoC; (2) appointment proof (letter) and the Kurdistan distinction; (3) a current regulated-product list with a date and a "check my HS code" option; (4) a document checklist; (5) a 4-step diagram including border verification and release; (6) registration (SoR) route for regular shippers; (7) indicative timelines; (8) an online RFC with file upload, or at least a PDF; (9) a country contact list for inspections at origin; (10) FAQs; (11) complaints/appeals and impartiality statements; (12) a way to verify a certificate's authenticity at destination.
- TIC already has items 2, 4, 5, 6 and 10 and could lead the mid-tier on Iraq content with modest additions (dated product list, timelines, online RFC, verification, Kurdistan note, consistent naming).

### Gaps
- No competitor page states COSQC fees or the official COSQC product list URL; I could not locate COSQC's own website for the ICIGI product list, so whether linking to it is possible is unknown.
- Whether TIC's COSQC appointment covers Kurdistan (KSQCA) is not stated on its page and was not found elsewhere.

- Making the ANAB ISO 17020, GAFTA and APEDA statuses verifiable (certificate number plus link to the ANAB directory and GAFTA register) would be a credibility step none of the mid-tier currently takes.
- Halal certification is a niche TIC can own online: no direct competitor in the set presents it.

### Gaps
- ESMA/MoIAT's current halal certification body list was not fetched (halal.ae link is from 2017 and may be dead).
- Whether TIC appears on the public ANAB directory and GAFTA register was not checked (would require those directories' search tools, which were not fetched).
