# TIC's website must earn the trust its accreditation already proves

*Research report for the TIC Quality Control website rebuild proposal. Compiled 22 October 2026 from fifteen research notes in `research_notes/TIC website rebuild research/` (site audit, rendered visual benchmark, competitor site reviews, user journeys, best-practice, taxonomy and trust pages, accreditation registers, service-line benchmarks, Arabic and China readiness, platform and cost options, discovery methods). CONTENT findings (what the site says, how it is organised, what visitors need) are kept separate from TECH findings (platform, hosting, messaging, admin, cost) so directors can comment on one without the other.*

## Bottom line

ticqc.ae is a **2024 WordPress/Elementor site running PHP 7.4 (end-of-life since November 2022)** whose homepage weighs **9.07 MB and takes 22-30 seconds to paint on mobile**, whose only "verification of conformity" link returns a 404, and which publishes nine accreditation and membership logos of which **only three could be verified in public registers** ([ticqc.ae](https://ticqc.ae/); [ANAB register](https://search.anab.org/)). The company behind it is more credible than the site: TIC holds an active **ANAB ISO/IEC 17020 Type A accreditation (AI-3314, valid to 17 October 2028)** covering Dubai, New Delhi, Amman, Guangzhou and Riyadh, a **GOEIC registration (record 73, June 2025)** and a **COSQC licence for Iraq CoC from September 2024**, and none of these facts appears on the site in verifiable form ([ANAB scope PDF](https://search.anab.org/public/organization_files/TIC-Quality-Control-LLC-Cert-and-Scope-File-10-16-2026_1792169068.pdf); [GOEIC record 73](https://www.goeic.gov.eg/ar/company-guide/registered-inspection-and-audit-companies/73)). The international majors (SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, the three TÜVs, DNV, LR, Applus+, UL, Kiwa, Element) converge on one grammar: a four-to-six-item header led by Services and Industries, click-to-open mega-menus, one static hero, one accent colour, breadcrumbs, a certificate-verification tool, a client portal, per-country programme pages with dated downloads and online request forms, and public impartiality and complaints documents. The direct programme competitors that matter most to TIC's Iraq and Egypt business (Cotecna, HQTS, Baltic Control, Sunchine, QIMA) all have a portal, certificate lookup and online request forms; **nobody in the set publishes fees, turnaround times or a human-staffed WhatsApp line**, which is TIC's opening. On the technology side none of the majors runs WordPress, but a disciplined modern WordPress or a headless CMS (Sanity, Payload, Wagtail) on managed hosting with WPML-grade Arabic, self-hosted fonts and a certificate lookup fed from TIC's own records is achievable within a **AED 90,000-250,000 build and five-to-six-month timeline** collected from UAE agency price pages. The single most important decision is not the CMS; it is whether TIC will publish only claims it can link to a register, name the people and offices behind each service, and commit a named owner per office to keep the content alive.

## CONTENT: ticqc.ae today is a brochure with the proof missing

The current site has **49 pages and 34 posts in its Yoast sitemap but only about 20 pages in the navigation**; around 25 pages are orphaned, including the whole Testing tree, the SFDA page (whose title is still "Elementor #2124"), Agri, Sustainability, eight product-inspection pages and ten ISO course pages ([ticqc.ae page sitemap](https://ticqc.ae/page-sitemap.xml)). The menu reads Home, About, Industries (Oil & Gas, Marine, Food, Commodities & Consumer products, Vehicle), Services (a "#" link whose dropdown holds GSP with Iraq CoC, Egypt CoC/CoI, Botswana CoC and Libya CoI; Business Assurance with Halal and CSUAS; and Training), News & Media and Contact. The homepage "Government Services Programs" card and a footer link both point to `/verification-of-conformity/`, which is a **404**. The 2024 industry pages run 350-650 words with no form and no FAQ; the 2026 programme pages run 1,500-5,000 words with FAQs (Iraq 23, Egypt 8, Botswana 8, CSUAS 8) and .docx request forms, but no page has an online application ([ticqc.ae Iraq CoC](https://ticqc.ae/iraq-coc/)). The Libya page is the only one that publishes a fee (0.6 percent of FOB value, minimum USD 350).

The About page has no founding year, no leadership names, no group structure and mentions "Seacorr" twice where "TIC Quality Control" is expected; the footer lists **14 addresses in 12 countries with phone numbers for only four, obfuscated emails and typos ("AdDresses", "Goverment", "King Fasial", "TIC Counsel")** ([ticqc.ae About](https://ticqc.ae/about-us/)). The homepage counters animate to "200+ / 900+ / 10+ / 10,000+" while a text block elsewhere says "15+ countries" and the footer shows 12. Missing entirely: a privacy policy, terms, copyright line, accreditation page, office pages, careers page, training booking, certificate verification, client login and any Arabic (the Polylang `/pt/` Portuguese site is an empty English shell). Security exposure is real: `/wp-json/wp/v2/users` lists five author accounts and `wp-login.php` is reachable without rate limiting. The site is behind Cloudflare, uses GTM-5KRPQDSK, Yoast, Complianz, Contact Form 7, Elementor Pro and GTranslate, and LinkedIn shows the company at **16,340 followers, founded 2020, 51-200 employees**, a scale the site does not convey ([TIC LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/tic-quality-control/)).

Visually, the rendered benchmark found the Elementor global kit still at factory defaults (#6EC1E4, Roboto/Roboto Slab) while the painted palette is charcoal #252324 and red #E42313, with **six font families in use** (Arial headings at 82 px, Poppins and Inter on the Iraq page, a system font on Contact), **20 of 23 homepage headings in capitals**, a 12-slide fading stock hero and a separate navy design system on the Iraq CoC page (18,230 px tall with a 51-slide carousel). Lighthouse mobile performance scored **53 (home), 58 (Iraq) and 62 (contact)**; the Iraq page has **204 colour-contrast failures** from one choice (grey text at 60-70 percent opacity on navy); Google's Chrome UX Report has **no field data at all for ticqc.ae**, meaning too few Chrome visitors to measure ([PageSpeed Insights, ticqc.ae mobile](https://pagespeed.web.dev/analysis/https-ticqc-ae/qzfa090qnl?form_factor=mobile)). The weight is almost entirely images (8.33 of 9.07 MB): one 3.6 MB hero JPEG and four accreditation logos scanned at 2,000+ pixels and displayed at 200.

## CONTENT: visitors want routes, documents, fees and proof, and an accredited body is obliged to publish some of it

The user-journey note identifies thirteen visitor types, of which five drive the Iraq and Egypt business: the exporter preparing a shipment, the importer or customs broker checking a certificate, the forwarder, the commodity trader looking for a GAFTA-approved superintendent, and the procurement officer prequalifying a supplier. What they need is concrete. Intertek and HQTS both publish the Iraq document list (RfC form, proforma invoice, product data sheet, evidence of compliance, transport document, L/C, certificate of origin) and HQTS states a timeline of 1-2 days for document review and about 5 days to certification; GOEIC imposes a 45-day rule and allows no amendments after submission; GAFTA tells traders to "look for the logo" of an approved superintendent ([Intertek Iraq](https://www.intertek.com/government/product-conformity/exports-to-iraq/); [GAFTA approved registers](https://www.gafta.com/approved-registers/)). The NN/g B2B research cited in the notes names hidden pricing as the most user-hostile omission on B2B sites and measured task success at 58 percent, and the 2026 vendor-website survey cited puts trust in vendor sites at 55 percent against 39 percent for chatbots, with only 18 percent of buyers using chatbots at all ([b2b-website-best-practice.md](../research_notes/TIC%20website%20rebuild%20research/b2b-website-best-practice.md)). Channel evidence is equally clear: WhatsApp penetration is about 90 percent in the UAE and 75 percent of nationals in Egypt and Saudi Arabia, and Chinese exporters rarely check email, so WeChat is the Guangzhou channel ([users-and-journeys.md](../research_notes/TIC%20website%20rebuild%20research/users-and-journeys.md)). Because **no body in the set publishes fees (except TIC's own Libya page), typical turnaround, or a human-staffed WhatsApp line**, a TIC programme page that does all three with a dated "last reviewed" line and a regulatory-updates feed would be the most useful page in the market. The recommended skeleton has twelve parts: regulatory summary with legal basis and TIC's appointment status; who must comply and the dated product list; routes with a decision helper; step-by-step process with timeline; document checklist with an online RfC; testing, inspection and labelling requirements; fees or a fee-structure PDF; FAQ; regulatory updates; contacts per office and a request form; complaints and appeals link; revision date and disclaimer. Every one of those parts is observed on at least one major's page.

On the obligations side, the standards are specific. **ISO/IEC 17021-1 clause 8.1.1 requires a management-system certification body to make six items public without request** (audit processes, granting and withdrawing certification, use of the name and mark, complaints and appeals, policies on impartiality, and how to obtain information), and IAF MD 28 requires CertSearch upload; **ISO/IEC 17065 clause 4.6 makes scheme, fee basis, rights and duties and complaints information available on request and clause 7.8 requires a certificate directory**; **ISO/IEC 17020 clause 4.1 requires impartiality, clause 7.5.2 a complaints process available on request, and clause 6.3 that subcontracting be disclosed with responsibility retained** ([ISO/IEC 17020:2012](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17020.2012.pdf); [ISO/IEC 17065:2012](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17065.2012.pdf)). Accreditation symbol rules bite directly on the current footer: EIAC-RQ-GEN-002 requires prior approval for website use, a 100 px minimum and the accreditation number under the logo, and GAFTA limits its logo to approved services ([EIAC symbol rules](https://www.bsigroup.com/siteassets/pdf/ar-ae/about-us/eiac-rq-gen-002-rev-7-eiac-accreditation-symbol-ilac-mra-iaf-mla.pdf); [GAFTA logo rules](https://www.gafta.com/approved-registers/gafta-approved-register-logo/)). Measured against those rules, the register check produced a sobering list. Verified: ANAB AI-3314 (with five sites and a scope that names COSQC SOPs, GAFTA 123/124 and oil and gas custody transfer), GOEIC record 73, TIC Council full membership and GAFTA approved superintendent for the Indian entity only ([ANAB register](https://search.anab.org/); [TIC Council members](https://www.tic-council.org/membership/members-directory); [GAFTA register](https://www.gafta.com/membership/approved-registers/?category=SUPR)). Partial: the COSQC licence (an official letter image, contract 5 of 22 May 2024, three-year term, renewal due September 2027, no public roster). Not found: ISO/IEC 17065 in EIAC's 976 entries, SAAC or MoIAT; NABL; the ISO 9001/14001/45001 badges (stock graphics with no certifier or number); APEDA; the "AIACI" logo, which is in fact the **EIAC logo displayed without any EIAC accreditation**; "Selo Associados" (not in the Arab Brazilian Chamber's 145-member list); the SFDA "designated body" claim (TIC is absent from SFDA's April 2026 list while GulfTIC, Intertek, TÜV Austria and QIMA are present); MoIAT halal registration (11 bodies, none TIC); and BOBS, CBL and KSQCA appointments, which have no public registers ([EIAC directory](https://eiac.gov.ae/directory/); [SFDA list, April 2026](https://sfda.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-04/LaboratoriesOffices.pdf); [MoIAT halal bodies](https://moiat.gov.ae/en/programs/halal/registered-halal-certification-bodies)). The practical consequence is that the accreditation page must be built as a register-linked table (body, standard, entity and sites, number, status, valid-until, verify link, PDF, last-checked), the pattern Kiwa and Baltic Control use ([Kiwa accreditations](https://www.kiwa.com/en/about-kiwa/accreditations-and-notifications/); [Baltic Control Certification](https://www.bccertification.com/about-us)), and that several current claims must be reworded or removed before launch, because a buyer, a bank or a GOEIC officer can run the same searches this research ran.

The service-line benchmarks sharpen the content plan page by page. Marine and oil-and-gas pages should be one short page per survey type naming the standard (ISO 13739 bunker, ISO 8217 fuel) with a request-a-survey form, after Intertek's petroleum cargo template; agri must be split from consumer products, use GAFTA wording and link the register entry; consumer-product inspection pages should follow the QIMA/Pro QC anatomy (IPC/DPI/PSI/CLC, ISO 2859-1 AQL, an ungated sample report, a booking form, 6-18 FAQs) ([QIMA PSI](https://www.qima.com/consumer-products/pre-shipment-inspection); [Pro QC PSI](https://proqc.com/pre-shipment-inspections/)); the food and testing pages must name the partner laboratory and its accreditation number because 17020 clause 6.3 requires subcontracting to be disclosed. The Kurdistan vehicle CoC deserves its own programme page, since Bureau Veritas's parallel Kurdistan VOCIV page is about 200 words with no form, no fees and an empty Downloads heading, so TIC can lead ([Verigates Kurdistan](https://verigates.bureauveritas.com/node/575)). Training pages can out-publish the majors simply by showing a catalogue with dates, prices, format and certificate issuer (no major landing page shows any), while carrying Intertek's impartiality sentence that training "is not for endorsing or recommending any particular methods" ([Intertek training](https://www.intertek.com/business-assurance/training/)); the halal page should follow the SGS Gulf datasheet order (mandate, scope by GSO 2055-2 category, standards, process, documents, fee basis, mark licence, verification by number, named manager) and must name an accreditation body or drop the 17065 claim ([SGS halal directory](https://halalcertificationdirectory.sgs.com/); [MoIAT halal](https://moiat.gov.ae/en/halal)); sustainability should be split into "Sustainability advisory" and "ESG assurance and verification" with a one-sentence independence statement that no peer page carries ([Control Union verification](https://www.controlunion.com/service/verification-validation/)); the CSUAS page must use QCI's own status vocabulary ("approved provisionally", "Statement of Conformity", "Form D-1 on eGCA") and not call TIC a certification body until approval ([SGS CSUAS process](https://www.sgs.com/-/media/sgscorp/documents/corporate/brochures/sgs-cp-certification-process-details-en.cdn.en-IN.pdf)). The Seacorr relationship should be presented as a group section with the ISO/IEC 17020 6.3 wording (TIC tells the client in advance and remains responsible for the conformity decision), modelled on Kiwa's statement that its owner SHV "does not interfere" in inspection decisions, since Seacorr's own site currently does not mention TIC at all and both sites share the same broken cookie-banner placeholders ([About Kiwa](https://www.kiwa.com/en/about-kiwa/); [Seacorr](https://www.seacorr.com/)).

## TECH: none of the majors runs WordPress, but TIC's real constraints are Arabic, China, verification and admin discipline

The majors run Sitecore (SGS, TÜV SÜD), Optimizely (Intertek, DNV, Kiwa, LR, Element), Drupal 11 (Bureau Veritas, UL) and Magnolia (Applus+); their direct mid-size competitors Cotecna, QIMA and Baltic Control run custom or Next.js stacks, while HQTS, Control Union and Pro QC run WordPress, HQTS on the same Elementor combination as TIC ([platform-and-tech-options.md](../research_notes/TIC%20website%20rebuild%20research/platform-and-tech-options.md)). The enterprise DXPs cost in the order of £50-80k a year in licences alone and are not a mid-size pattern. Three realistic packages emerged. Package A is a **rebuilt WordPress on managed hosting** (Kinsta from about USD 35 to 375 a month with a WAF on all plans, or WP Engine from USD 28) with a locked block library, WPML (EUR 99), ACF Pro and Gravity Forms, which keeps the in-house lead's existing skills and the largest agency pool but inherits WordPress's attack surface: Patchstack recorded **11,334 WordPress vulnerabilities in 2025, 91 percent in plugins** ([Kinsta plans](https://kinsta.com/plans/); [WP Engine plans](https://wpengine.com/plans/); [Patchstack whitepapers](https://patchstack.com/whitepaper/)). Package B is a **headless CMS plus a static or server-rendered front end**: Sanity (Growth at USD 15 per seat, unlimited locales), Storyblok (USD 99 a month, two locales included, workflows only on Premium), Payload (MIT licence, workflows at Enterprise tier) or Strapi, with Next.js or Astro on Vercel or Cloudflare, which gives the best performance, RTL and China control but depends on a JavaScript-capable maintainer ([Sanity pricing](https://www.sanity.io/pricing); [Storyblok pricing](https://www.storyblok.com/pricing); [Payload](https://payloadcms.com/)). Package C is **Wagtail**, the BSD-licensed Django CMS with built-in workflows, permissions, audit log, scheduling and a WCAG 2.2 AA admin, which is the strongest choice only if the in-house lead is Python-first ([Wagtail](https://wagtail.org/)). The note is explicit that **the in-house lead's available hours over six months and Python/JavaScript depth is the single most important unknown**; every platform recommendation depends on it, and it should be asked in person before the wider questionnaire round.

Three technical requirements are non-negotiable whatever the CMS. First, **certificate verification**: a number-only lookup with rate limiting, no enumeration, a QR code printed on each certificate pointing at the lookup, and a nightly sync from wherever TIC's certificate records live (unknown today). Every competitor that matters has one, DNV's and Bureau Veritas's print QR codes, and the Kurdistan, Iraq and Egypt journeys all begin with a customs broker or bank wanting to confirm a certificate ([DNV certificate checker](https://certificatechecker.dnv.com/); [Intertek directories](https://intertek.com/directories)). Second, **Arabic as a first-class locale**, not a GTranslate overlay: `dir="rtl"` at the document level, a self-hosted Arabic typeface, numbers and phone numbers left-to-right, mirrored direction icons only, reciprocal hreflang with x-default, and translation review in Cairo, Baghdad or Amman using Modern Standard Arabic; W3C's Arabic layout requirements and Dubai Chamber's and Emirates NBD's bilingual sites are the local references ([W3C Arabic layout requirements](https://www.w3.org/TR/alreq/); [Dubai Chamber Arabic](https://www.dubaichambercommerce.com/ar/)). UAE agency rates run AED 0.30-0.40 per word, so an Arabic layer of about 20,000 words costs roughly AED 6-8k plus 15-30 percent a year to maintain ([Lingo translation rates](https://lingo.ae/how-much-does-a-translation-agency-charge/)). The Arabic-first pages are the verification tool, the destination-inspection explainer, office contacts, complaints and appeals, impartiality, privacy and the about and accreditation pages; exporter guides stay English and Chinese. Third, **China reachability** for the Guangzhou office's exporter clients: ticqc.ae itself loaded from all five mainland test locations, but Google Fonts, Google Tag Manager, Google Analytics, Google Maps, reCAPTCHA, YouTube, LinkedIn, wa.me, vercel.app, pages.dev and the AWS me-central-1 and Azure UAE North endpoints did not, while cdnjs, jsDelivr, unpkg, hCaptcha and challenges.cloudflare.com did; Cloudflare states that Turnstile and Pages are not available in the mainland ([Cloudflare China Network FAQ](https://developers.cloudflare.com/china-network/faq/)). The implication is to self-host fonts and scripts, use hCaptcha or server-side anti-spam instead of reCAPTCHA, avoid YouTube embeds and Google Maps on exporter pages, and test staging from China before launch. An ICP filing is required only if the domain resolves to mainland servers, so a Hong Kong or Singapore edge with a Dubai origin needs none ([Alibaba Cloud ICP filing](https://www.alibabacloud.com/product/icp-filing)); every major with a Chinese site (Bureau Veritas, SGS, TÜV SÜD, Intertek, Cotecna) instead runs a `.cn` domain with ICP and public-security numbers, Baidu Tongji, a 400 hotline and a WeChat official account, which TIC can defer until Guangzhou's entity status and demand are known ([Bureau Veritas China](https://www.bureauveritas.cn/); [Intertek China](https://www.intertek.com.cn/)). A WeChat Service Account for an overseas entity is reported at USD 99 a year ([AppInChina WeChat guide](https://appinchina.co/blog/how-to-open-a-wechat-official-account-step-by-step-guide/)).

Messaging and lead handling should be designed around channels people in TIC's markets already use. A **click-to-WhatsApp button is free**; the WhatsApp Business Platform charges per message with 1,000 free service conversations a month; Intercom runs USD 29-132 per seat, Crisp USD 0-295 a month, tawk.to is free; a Zbooni/YouGov survey cited in the notes found 85 percent of UAE consumers prefer WhatsApp and nine in ten prefer a human, which matches the 39 percent trust in chatbots above and argues for **human-staffed WhatsApp with published hours rather than a bot** ([platform-and-tech-options.md](../research_notes/TIC%20website%20rebuild%20research/platform-and-tech-options.md)). Forms should carry 5-7 fields with country-to-office routing and a fallback to Dubai, feed a CRM (Zoho, which opened UAE data centres in January 2026, at roughly USD 14-20 per user, or HubSpot), and send through a transactional email provider with SPF, DKIM and DMARC so that replies from 13 offices do not land in spam. Hosting in AWS me-central-1 or Azure UAE North is available, but neither the UAE PDPL (Federal Decree-Law 45/2021) nor Saudi PDPL mandates in-country hosting for a marketing site; what does need checking is whether the Iraq and Egypt programme contracts carry residency clauses, a question only the GSP director can answer. Consent management (Cookiebot at USD 8-56 a month versus OneTrust at about USD 10k a year) and a privacy policy covering GDPR, UAE PDPL and Saudi PDPL opt-in are required pages the site lacks today. Keep ticqc.ae as the primary domain unless the directors choose ticqc.com; either way every one of the 83 sitemap URLs and the uploads need a 301 map kept for at least a year, and 12 months of GA4 and Search Console data should be exported before anything changes.

Admin structure is where the proposal must be most concrete for the directors. The recommended role model has six roles (Global Admin, Content Manager, Office Editor, Reviewer, Translator, Viewer), a locked set of 15-25 content blocks so that no office can paint its own Iraq-style design system, page templates per type (service, programme, office, course, news, policy), tiered approvals with a "review-by" date on every programme page, and a hub-and-spoke governance with a named owner and deputy per office; TÜV NORD's nine fragmented logins and TÜV SÜD's and Bureau Veritas's neglected Middle East sub-sites are the cautionary examples of what happens when the spokes drift ([TÜV SÜD Middle East](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-ae); [Bureau Veritas Middle East](https://middle-east.bureauveritas.com/)). Costs collected from UAE, UK and Indian agency pages put a multilingual group site at **AED 90,000-250,000 (USD 25-68k)** for the build, with running costs of USD 600-6,000 a year hosting, USD 2-12k CRM, AED 30-72k retainer and AED 20-55k content and translation, on a timeline of about five to six months (discovery 2-3 weeks, design 4-6, build 8-10, migration and Arabic 3-4, QA 2) ([platform-and-tech-options.md](../research_notes/TIC%20website%20rebuild%20research/platform-and-tech-options.md)). Performance targets are Google's Core Web Vitals thresholds at the 75th percentile (LCP 2.5 s, INP 200 ms, CLS 0.1) and WCAG 2.2 AA, which the current site cannot meet while its hero alone is 3.6 MB ([web.dev Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)).

## Conclusion

The research reverses the usual order of a rebuild. TIC's problem is not that its site looks older than SGS's; it is that the site makes claims the public registers do not support while hiding the claims the registers do support, and that a GOEIC officer, a Saudi procurement team or a Chinese exporter's forwarder can discover both in ten minutes. An accredited Type A inspection body with an active ANAB scope that names COSQC procedures, GAFTA rules and custody transfer has a stronger story than any of its mid-size competitors tell, and the cheapest, fastest win in this entire programme is to tell it in verifiable form: one accreditation table, one group statement, one complaints page, one certificate lookup. Everything visual follows from discipline rather than budget, since the three sites that set the achievable bar (Kiwa, Element, Cotecna) did nothing more exotic than choose one typeface, one colour, one hero and sentence case and then apply them everywhere.

The second reversal concerns where TIC can lead rather than follow. The majors have left two gaps in exactly the markets TIC serves: Arabic is absent from SGS, Intertek, Cotecna and Bureau Veritas while every regulator TIC answers to is Arabic-first, and nobody publishes fees, turnaround or a human on WhatsApp. A programme page that states the route, the documents, the fee, the days and the named person in Baghdad or Cairo, in English, Arabic and Chinese, with a dated revision line, would be the most useful page in the Iraq and Egypt conformity market, and TIC already publishes more FAQ content on Iraq than any major. The platform choice should wait for one conversation with the in-house lead and one with the quality manager; the content decisions in the table above should not wait at all.
