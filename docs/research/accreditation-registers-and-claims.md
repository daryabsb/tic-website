## Key question 1: Accreditation bodies (ANAB 17020, ISO/IEC 17065, NABL 17025, ISO 9001/14001/45001 certifier)

### Takeaway
The ANAB ISO/IEC 17020 accreditation is real, current and richly documented on ANAB's public directory (certificate AI-3314, Type A, expiry 17 October 2028, scope v005 of 16 October 2026 covering Dubai plus satellites in New Delhi, Guangzhou, Riyadh and Amman); it is the single strongest verifiable asset for the new site and none of its details appear on ticqc.ae today. The ISO/IEC 17065, NABL ISO/IEC 17025 and ISO 9001/14001/45001 claims could not be verified anywhere: no accreditation body is named on the site, the three "ISO certificate" images are generic stock badges rather than certificates, and TIC does not appear in the EIAC, MoIAT, SAAC or (page 1 of) GAC directories.

### Inferences
- The India (2025-03) and Saudi/China (2025-11) "accreditation" news items most plausibly correspond to the addition of satellite locations under the single ANAB certificate AI-3314 (the directory shows one certificate with four satellites, not separate certificates); the website should describe them as "locations covered by ANAB accreditation AI-3314", not as separate accreditations.
- The scope's "Accreditation Granted: 16 October 2026" conflicts with TIC's 1 November 2024 announcement; the most likely reading is that v005 records a re-accreditation decision date, but only ANAB or TIC's quality manager can confirm the initial grant date.
- Offices not on the ANAB scope (Sharjah, Baghdad, Noida, Gandhidham, Lahore, Cairo, São Paulo, Maharagama, Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur) must not be shown next to the ANAB symbol as accredited sites; note 1 allows services "based out of" accredited offices to be provided elsewhere.
- The ISO/IEC 17065 claim is unverified in every directory reachable (EIAC complete, SAAC keyword, MoIAT complete, GAC page 1 only, ANAB product-certification directory unreachable); until the accreditation body and number are produced it should not be shown as "accredited".
- The ISO 9001/14001/45001 badges are not evidence; a bank or regulator cannot verify them, and badge graphics of this type are commonly sold as stock art.

### Gaps
- ANAB's management-system / product-certification directory on anab.ansi.org (separate from search.anab.org) returned HTTP 403 / Cloudflare "Just a moment" to every fetch, so an ANAB ISO/IEC 17065 accreditation could not be excluded.
- GAC directory pages 2–10 (about 419 entries) could not be read; the GAC keyword search did not apply via curl.
- NABL's directory (nabl-india.org) was not opened (search budget exhausted; directory is JS-driven); the NABL claim is unverified rather than disproved.
- No certification body for ISO 9001/14001/45001 could be identified; IAF CertSearch not queried.

### Inferences
- The GAFTA logo may be shown for the Indian entity's approved services only (GAFTA's permission "is limited to the Approved services of the member"); showing it site-wide under the Dubai company overstates the approval unless the Dubai entity is also approved under a name not found.
- "AIACI" is almost certainly an internal misnaming of "EIAC"; because no EIAC accreditation was found, the logo is either (a) for an accreditation held under a different legal name, (b) for an application in progress, or (c) an error. It must be removed or substantiated before launch.
- The CCAB seal is a chamber-of-commerce membership mark, not an accreditation; if membership exists under the Brazilian entity, it belongs in a "Memberships" row, not among accreditations.

### Gaps
- GAFTA's full member profile (membership number, approval date, scope, audit status) is behind login; the Indian entity's exact approved scope is unknown.
- APEDA lists for other schemes (organic, peanut labs/inspection agencies, registered exporters) were not opened; the basis of the APEDA logo is unknown.
- CCAB membership under a Brazilian entity name (e.g. "TIC Quality Control do Brasil") could not be checked because the list shows trade names only.

## Key question 4: Logo and claim rules per body

### Takeaway
Every accreditation body requires the symbol to be ANAB/EIAC-supplied, tied to an accreditation number and limited to the accredited scope; EIAC explicitly requires prior approval for website use and a minimum 100-pixel size; GAFTA limits its logo to the approved services of the approved member; ISO 9001-type certifier marks are governed by the certifier's own rules (unknown here because no certifier is identified); TIC Council publishes no logo rule in its public terms.

### Cited Findings
- ANAB PR 1018 (ANAB's documents page is behind Cloudflare; the current version is cited by an accredited body as "Policy on Use of ANAB Accreditation Symbols and Claims of Accreditation Status - PR 1018 from 10.02.2025"). Rules as transposed in SII's PAD 12.50 work instruction (dated 01/11/2025): "The accredited certification program number shall be printed with the accreditation mark"; display "only in the appropriate form, size, and color according the rules published by ANAB and using the mark provided by ANAB"; to refer to accreditation without the mark use "An ANAB accredited Certifier - Accreditation Number, without variation"; on letterhead "it will clearly identify the specific scope for which it is accredited"; "shall not use the ANAB mark in any misleading manner, or imply ... that ANAB specifically approves of its products or services"; "shall claim accreditation only with respect to the scopes for which it has been granted"; "All quotations that contain the ANAB mark or refer to ANAB accreditation shall clearly define the specific activities/programs that are ANAB accredited"; the mark "shall not be displayed on any product and packages"; on withdrawal "immediately cease to issue certificates and any other materials displaying the ANAB accreditation mark"; "shall not make any modifications to the ANAB accreditation mark"; mark size "2.75 inch width, 2.35 inch height", minimum 300 dpi, one or two colours (black, or blue and red, or white on dark). — [SII PAD 12.50](https://www.sii.org.il/media/n04n4jy5/pad-1250-use-of-the-anab-mark.pdf)
- ANAB continuation-of-accreditation letters state the symbol "must not be used in any way that implies accreditation outside the scope of accreditation"; ANAB offers a combined ANAB/ILAC mark on request. — [ANAB continuation letter example](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/241024-sdpdcl-cont-of-accr.pdf); [ANAB combined mark request](https://anab.ansi.org/combined-mark-request)
- ILAC P8:11/2023 (already extracted in the taxonomy notes): accredited CABs may claim accreditation on websites, but the claim must not mislead as to which activities are covered; proposals must distinguish accredited from non-accredited activities; the symbol must not imply the AB approves a product. — [ILAC P8](https://acreditare.md/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/ILAC_P8_11_2023.pdf)
- EIAC-RQ-GEN-002 Issue 1 Rev. 07 (27-11-2024): "The use of EIAC symbol is compulsory for all CABs accredited by EIAC, and shall be restricted to the accredited scopes only"; the symbol shall "Consist of the EIAC Logo in conjunction with the CABs accreditation number which is printed centrally under the EIAC logo"; shall not "appear in a size that makes the EIAC name unreadable ... for web as 100 pixels", "be displayed in a more prominent way than the logo of the accredited CAB", or "be used in such a way as to state, imply or suggest that EIAC accepts responsibility of any of the statements of conformity/approval"; 6.2: the symbol "can be used, subject to prior approval from the related department, by accredited Conformity Assessment Bodies on advertising/publicity materials mentioned below: ... h) Websites i) Social media ... k) Emails (Template Signature)"; 6.1: "shall not be used in any way that might mislead the reader about the status of an accredited CAB"; 5: during suspension/withdrawal "immediately discontinue the use of the accreditation symbols and any references to its accreditation status within the affected scope; in all ... marketing and promotional materials, stationeries, internet websites, social programs etc."; 7.2 e) the Accredited CAB Combined ILAC MRA/IAF MLA Mark may be used on "Online applications (websites, newsletters, email signatures etc.)" after signing the EIAC agreement; "The certified clients of certification bodies are not allowed to use Accredited CAB Combined IAF MLA Mark." — [EIAC-RQ-GEN-002 Rev 7](https://www.bsigroup.com/siteassets/pdf/ar-ae/about-us/eiac-rq-gen-002-rev-7-eiac-accreditation-symbol-ilac-mra-iaf-mla.pdf)
- UKAS comparator (from taxonomy notes): symbol on websites only where "the extent and limitations of the accreditation held is clearly described", e.g. "Our accreditation is limited to those activities described on our UKAS schedule of accreditation", with a link to the schedule. — [UKAS FAQs](https://www.ukas.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/UKAS-brand-website-FAQs.pdf)
- GAFTA Approved Register logo: "Gafta members who have met the requirements of the Gafta Approved Registers are able to use the Gafta Approved Logos" to "promote their services applicable to their membership and Approved status"; "The permission to use the logo(s) is limited to the Approved services of the member and may not be transferred or licenced to any other part of the business or to any other business"; "Immediate action will be taken against any misuse of the logo(s)" or "false claim regarding the Approved Registers or Approved Register Logos"; apply via ApprovedRegisters@gafta.com. — [GAFTA logo page](https://www.gafta.com/approved-registers/gafta-approved-register-logo/)
- TIC Council: the public Terms & conditions (14 pages) contain no member-logo clause; the compliance page sets code obligations only. — [TIC Council T&C](https://www.tic-council.org/download_file/178/1)
- GAC: the GAC "documents" portal lists controlled-document categories but exposes no symbol-use document without login. — [GAC documents](https://www.gcc-accreditation.org/documents)
- CCAB seal: usable "on all of the associate's communication materials, such as business cards, promotional folders, websites, and banners" (ANBA report of the chamber's practice). — [ANBA](https://anba.com.br/en/arab-brazilian-chamber-seeks-new-associates/)
- ISO/IEC 17065 4.1.3 and 17021-1 8.1.1 d) require the CB itself to control and publish rules for use of its own certification mark (TIC already posts "TIC-UCM-01 Use of Certification Mark" and "Rules for Use of Certification Mark"). — [ISO/IEC 17065](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/iec/iec.17065.2012.pdf); [CSUAS page downloads](https://ticqc.ae/csuas/)

### Inferences
- Required by rule next to the ANAB symbol on the new site: the ANAB-supplied symbol unaltered, certificate number AI-3314, the standard (ISO/IEC 17020:2012) and a statement limiting the claim to the scope, with a link to the ANAB directory entry or the scope PDF; prohibited: placing the symbol on pages describing non-accredited services (halal, CSUAS, training, Libya/Botswana programmes unless in scope) or implying ANAB approves inspected goods.
- Common practice (not rule): a one-line disclaimer under each accreditation card, downloadable certificate, "verify on the register" link, and a last-verified date.
- The EIAC logo must be removed unless TIC holds an EIAC accreditation and has EIAC's prior approval for website display.

### Gaps
- ANAB PR 1018's own text (version of 10 February 2025) was not retrieved; the clauses above are a licensee's transposition and should be checked against the original before the brand guide is written.
- GAC's symbol-use document and TIC Council's logo guidance (if any, perhaps in the members' extranet) were not accessible.

## Key question 5: Comparators — how similar bodies present verified accreditations

### Takeaway
Kiwa is the best model for a register-linked accreditations table (number linked to the AB register, type, standard, entity, location, search, disclaimer); Cotecna shows a prose page with certificate PDFs and a separate certificate-authentication tool; Baltic Control (closest to TIC in size and scope) has no accreditation page at all and relies on news posts and footer logos, which is the pattern TIC should move away from.

### Cited Findings
- Kiwa "Accreditations and notifications": each entry shows "Registration number, which links to the accreditation or notification body's register", accreditation type (persons certification, management system certification, notified body, inspection body, testing laboratory), directive/standard, entity, location; register links go to SWEDAC, DAkkS, EU NANDO, FINAS, IANZ, NATA, ESDA, IPAC; search box and "Load more"; disclaimer that the list "is updated periodically but may be incomplete" and users are "directed to the accreditation and notification bodies' websites for complete information". Example entries: "0558 UK Approved Body Directive/Standard Gas Appliance Regulation Kiwa Energy Cheltenham, United Kingdom"; "A0018 Management System Certification Directive/Standard ISO/IEC 17021 Kiwa Sativa Lisbon, Portugal"; "PERS 003 Persons Certification Directive/Standard ISO/IEC 17024 Kiwa Teknologisk Institutt Sertifisering AS Oslo, Norway". — [Kiwa accreditations](https://www.kiwa.com/en/about-kiwa/accreditations-and-notifications/)
- Cotecna "Certifications and Accreditations": prose grouped as Group Certification (ISO 9001, multi-site), Group Accreditations ("Cotecna implements an Inspection Management System (IMS) accredited to ISO / IEC 17020", including the GAFTA Approved Superintendent scope), Other Accreditations (17025, 17065, 17021), Group Memberships (TIC Council, Transparency International Switzerland, Swiss-African Business Circle, FOSFA, GAFTA, SAL, IFA, ICA, FCC); "Related Documents": Quality Policy (147 KB), ISO 9001 Certificate (0.7 MB), ISO/IEC 17020 Certificate (0.44 MB); no certificate numbers or dates on the page; separate "Certificate Authentication" page in navigation. — [Cotecna certifications](https://www.cotecna.com/en/about/certifications-and-accreditations)
- Baltic Control: no accreditations page; accreditations appear as news items ("Baltic Control Misr in Egypt is now accredited ISO/IEC 17020:2012 by EGAC in three interesting business areas"; "Baltic Control India is now accredited ISO/IEC 17065:2012 by NABCB"; appointments for Iraq, GOEIC, SIRIM, KSO Sucofindo, PNGRB, APEDA, NAFED) and footer logos linking to tic-council.org, fosfa.org and gafta.com; ANAB's directory shows Baltic Control A/S, AI-3293, Bulk Cargo, through 03/21/2028 with Poland and UK satellites. — [Baltic Control](https://balticcontrol.com/); [ANAB directory](https://search.anab.org/)
- SGS Gulf halal directory (from taxonomy notes): "Track Your Halal Certificate" by number, "All Certificate" listing, statement of MoIAT approval and SASO/SFDA recognition. — [SGS halal directory](https://halalcertificationdirectory.sgs.com/)
- Register-side presentation worth mirroring: ANAB's record (certificate number, standard, categories, status, effective-through date, satellites, cert+scope PDF); EIAC's (accreditation number, category, initial accreditation date, status); GAC's (number, country, valid-until); MoIAT's (AB, issue/expiry, status, two certificate downloads); GOEIC's (name, logo, contact, scope PDF). — sources as cited in KQ1–3

### Inferences
- A TIC accreditations page that lists, per row: body, standard/scheme, legal entity and sites covered, number, status, valid-until, "verify" link to the register, certificate/scope PDF, and a last-checked date would exceed Cotecna and Baltic Control and match Kiwa.
- Because TIC's ANAB record carries satellites, the page should show a site matrix (Dubai, New Delhi, Guangzhou, Riyadh, Amman) rather than one logo.

### Gaps
- Intertek's accreditations page (intertek.com/about/accreditations/) returned 404; SGS pages return 403; Bureau Veritas was covered in other notes.
